## Problem

Design a cab-booking service. Riders A and B both request a trip near driver D1. Both may see D1 on a map, but only one may receive that driver's accepted assignment.

Now suppose D1 receives A's offer O1, misses its deadline, and is offered B's trip under O2. A delayed timer or acceptance for O1 must not take D1 away from O2. This is the core design problem: matching can propose a driver, but one authority must decide who owns the driver and trip.

## Requirements

I will solve one city's core dispatch and trip lifecycle with these choices:

- Each rider has at most one live request/trip. A stable rider-scoped request ID returns the same trip on retry.
- Match the requested vehicle category to an available driver within 5 km whose latest server-observed position is less than ten seconds old.
- Use one sequential, exclusive pending offer per driver and per trip. An offer lasts 15 seconds, within an overall two-minute search deadline. A declined/timed-out driver is not offered that same request again.
- Acceptance changes both the trip assignment and driver ownership together. Expiry, decline and cancellation check the exact offer/trip identity.
- Progress through `SEARCHING → OFFERED → ASSIGNED → ARRIVED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`. Decline/expiry can return an offered trip to search. A rider confirms start after arrival.
- Cancellation is free before arrival and ₹50 after arrival, using the tariff frozen for that trip. It is unavailable through this ordinary operation after start. The rider approves the current trip version and displayed cancellation amount.
- Calculate an estimated fare from a route estimate, then a final fare from trusted distance evidence and elapsed trip time. Freeze the tariff. Completion and cancellation amounts are debts/receipts of assessment, not proof of payment.

The runnable example uses a synthetic flat city plane, a pure local route-estimate fixture and cash fare assessment. Real maps/ETA, authentication, meter attestation, payment networks, pooling, ratings, emergency handling and driver-initiated cancellation are outside this mechanism. Matching by straight-line distance is deliberately not a claim to find the fastest road pickup.

## 1. Separate a nearby driver, an offer and an assignment

| Fact | Example | What it allows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location candidate | D1 was observed 100 metres from A. | Consider D1 during matching; it grants no ownership. |
| Pending offer | O1 invites D1 to take T1 until t=15 seconds. | Reserve D1's offer slot exclusively while waiting. |
| Accepted assignment | D1 accepts current O1 before its deadline. | T1 owns D1 and D1 is unavailable to another trip. |
| Historical trip | T1 has completed. | Keep its fare and evidence, while D1 may now own T2. |

An offer is not an accepted passenger trip, but it is exclusive in this selected policy. D1 cannot receive B's O2 while O1 remains pending. This avoids a fanout race in the first implementation. The cost is waiting for a decline or deadline before trying another driver.

[![Dispatch service ranks fresh location candidates and jointly controls Driver slot, Trip state and expiring Offer identity. The trip retains frozen fare terms and measured evidence.](designs/lld-7.svg)](designs/lld-7.svg)

The driver slot has four states: `OFFLINE`, `AVAILABLE`, `OFFERED` and `ON_TRIP`. `ON_TRIP` starts at assignment, including the drive to pickup. It remains owned until that trip is completed or a permitted cancellation releases it.

## 2. Give each object a clear responsibility

| Object | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `Driver` | Category, latest position and one current offer or trip slot. |
| `Trip` | Rider request, legal progress, current driver, version, tariff and final evidence. |
| `Offer` | Bind one trip and driver with a unique token, deadline and disposition. |
| `Estimator` | Supply a route estimate. The included fixture is a pure bounded function. |
| `Tariff` | Immutable values used by estimate, final fare and cancellation. |
| `Service` | Coordinate driver/trip transitions, request replay and immutable event intents. |

The service must preserve these invariants:

- A live driver slot belongs to at most one pending offer or one assigned trip.
- A live assigned trip has one driver, and that driver's current trip points back to it.
- A pending offer is current on both its trip and driver. Old tokens cannot release newer ownership.
- A rider has at most one nonterminal trip. Retrying its original request cannot create another.
- Progress and fare evidence are retained. An old completion cannot free a driver's later trip.
- The cancellation version and fee belong to the same observed trip state.

One service lock protects this small model. We are not using a location index, Java `compareAndSet` on a single driver field or a notification consumer as an alternative owner. Changing only the driver and forgetting the trip would still violate the second invariant.

## 3. Select a candidate, then claim the offer slot

Matching scans available drivers of the right category, filters positions by freshness and radius, and picks the shortest straight-line distance. Driver ID breaks ties deterministically. Squared distance preserves this ranking without calculating square roots.

Position updates carry a sequence number; an old sequence cannot overwrite a newer observation. The service records observation time itself. This does not authenticate a physical location; a real telemetry adapter owns that trust boundary. Freshness is checked when selecting a candidate, not treated as a lease that later cancels an accepted passenger trip.

Issuing O1 writes both sides under the same lock:

```text
Driver D1: AVAILABLE -> OFFERED, current offer O1
Trip T1:   SEARCHING -> OFFERED, current offer O1
Offer O1:  PENDING, expires at min(now+15s, request search deadline)
```

If there is no eligible candidate, return no offer and leave the trip searching until its deadline. The dispatcher may retry as fresh candidates arrive. It must not loop forever or keep re-offering a driver who already declined that request.

### Accept the exact current offer

Acceptance verifies the driver identity, pending state, expiry and both ownership references. Only then does it make D1 `ON_TRIP` for T1 and T1 `ASSIGNED` to D1. A replayed accepted O1 returns T1's current view; it is not permission to assign that driver again.

```java
public synchronized Optional<OfferView> offerNext(String tripId) {
    Trip t = trip(tripId);
    expireSearch(tripId);
    if (t.state == TripState.CANCELLED) return Optional.empty();
    if (t.state != TripState.SEARCHING) throw new IllegalStateException("Trip already has an offer or assignment");
    Instant now = now();
    Optional<Driver> candidate = drivers.values().stream()
        .filter(d -> d.state == DriverState.AVAILABLE && d.category == t.command.category()
            && !t.triedDrivers.contains(d.id) && !d.observedAt.isAfter(now)
            && Duration.between(d.observedAt, now).compareTo(GPS_AGE) < 0
            && d.point.distanceSquared(t.command.pickup()) <= 25_000_000L)
        .min(Comparator.comparingLong((Driver d) -> d.point.distanceSquared(t.command.pickup())).thenComparing(d -> d.id));
    if (candidate.isEmpty()) return Optional.empty();
    Driver d = candidate.get();
    Instant until = now.plus(OFFER_LIFE);
    if (t.searchUntil.isBefore(until)) until = t.searchUntil;
    Offer offer = new Offer("O" + ++offerSequence, t.id, d.id, until);
    offers.put(offer.id, offer); t.triedDrivers.add(d.id);
    d.state = DriverState.OFFERED; d.offerId = offer.id; t.offerId = offer.id;
    transition(t, TripState.OFFERED, "exclusive offer issued");
    return Optional.of(offer.view());
}

public synchronized Result accept(String offerId, String driverId) {
    Offer offer = ownedOffer(offerId, driverId);
    Trip t = trip(offer.tripId); Driver d = driver(driverId);
    if (offer.state == OfferState.ACCEPTED) return new Result(Decision.REPLAYED, t.view());
    if (offer.state != OfferState.PENDING) return new Result(Decision.NOT_CURRENT, t.view());
    if (!now().isBefore(offer.expiresAt)) {
        expireOffer(offer.id);
        return new Result(Decision.EXPIRED, t.view());
    }
    if (t.state != TripState.OFFERED || !offer.id.equals(t.offerId)
        || d.state != DriverState.OFFERED || !offer.id.equals(d.offerId))
        return new Result(Decision.NOT_CURRENT, t.view());
    offer.state = OfferState.ACCEPTED;
    t.offerId = null; t.driverId = d.id;
    d.offerId = null; d.tripId = t.id; d.state = DriverState.ON_TRIP;
    transition(t, TripState.ASSIGNED, "driver and trip assigned together");
    return new Result(Decision.ACCEPTED, t.view());
}
```

![A driver candidate becomes one exclusive pending offer. Acceptance checks its token and deadline, then commits Trip ASSIGNED and Driver ON_TRIP together. A location hit alone never grants assignment.](learning-figures/lld-7-assignment.svg)

In a durable adapter, the relevant trip, driver slot and offer must be checked and committed in one supported transaction boundary. A conditional update on the driver alone cannot also prove the trip was not cancelled. All paths that change these facts must follow a consistent locking/transaction protocol.

## 4. Expiry and cancellation must preserve the current owner

Follow D1 across two requests:

| Time | Event | Driver ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| t=0 | T1 offers O1, deadline t=15. | D1 is exclusively `OFFERED` to T1. |
| t=15 | O1 expires at equality. | D1 becomes available; T1 returns to search. |
| t=15 | Fresh location received; T2 offers O2. | D1 is now `OFFERED` to T2. |
| Later | Old O1 timer/acceptance arrives. | O1 is no longer pending/current; O2 remains intact. |
| Before O2 deadline | D1 accepts O2. | D1 and T2 become assigned together. |

The release helper compares the expected offer ID on **both** records. It never says “driver is OFFERED, therefore set AVAILABLE” without checking which offer owns that state.

```java
public synchronized boolean decline(String offerId, String driverId) {
    Offer o = ownedOffer(offerId, driverId);
    if (o.state != OfferState.PENDING) return false;
    retire(o, OfferState.DECLINED, true); return true;
}
public synchronized boolean expireOffer(String offerId) {
    Offer o = offer(offerId);
    if (o.state != OfferState.PENDING || now().isBefore(o.expiresAt)) return false;
    retire(o, OfferState.EXPIRED, true); expireSearch(o.tripId); return true;
}
public synchronized boolean expireSearch(String tripId) {
    Trip t = trip(tripId);
    if ((t.state != TripState.SEARCHING && t.state != TripState.OFFERED) || now().isBefore(t.searchUntil)) return false;
    if (t.offerId != null) retire(offer(t.offerId), OfferState.CANCELLED, false);
    transition(t, TripState.CANCELLED, "search deadline expired without assignment");
    riderActive.remove(t.riderId, t.id); return true;
}
private void retire(Offer o, OfferState state, boolean resetTrip) {
    Driver d = driver(o.driverId); Trip t = trip(o.tripId);
    if (d.state == DriverState.OFFERED && o.id.equals(d.offerId)) {
        d.offerId = null; d.state = DriverState.AVAILABLE;
    }
    if (t.state == TripState.OFFERED && o.id.equals(t.offerId)) {
        t.offerId = null;
        if (resetTrip) transition(t, TripState.SEARCHING, "offer retired: " + state);
    }
    o.state = state;
}
```

A timer may run late, but acceptance independently checks its cutoff; a late timer cannot make an expired offer acceptable. Conversely, a missing timer can temporarily occupy a stale offer slot and reduce availability. A real dispatcher needs an expiry worker/recovery scan, while keeping the same identity guards.

![O1 expires, then D1 receives O2 for another trip. Old O1 events fail their identity/state checks. Cancellation and acceptance consult the same trip version and ownership boundary rather than leaving an orphan driver.](learning-figures/lld-7-offer-tokens.svg)

### Cancel against the state the rider approved

The trip view includes its version and current cancellation quote. Before arrival the quote is ₹0. Arrival changes the version and quotes ₹50. If the rider's old screen sends a cancellation for the earlier version, return `STALE_VERSION` without cancelling or assessing a fee. The rider can then approve the new state deliberately.

```java
public synchronized Result cancel(String tripId, String riderId, int expectedVersion) {
    Trip t = riderTrip(tripId, riderId);
    if (t.state == TripState.CANCELLED) return new Result(Decision.REPLAYED, t.view());
    if (t.state == TripState.IN_PROGRESS || t.state == TripState.COMPLETED)
        throw new IllegalStateException("Use trip completion or dispute workflow");
    if (t.version != expectedVersion) return new Result(Decision.STALE_VERSION, t.view());
    if (t.offerId != null) retire(offer(t.offerId), OfferState.CANCELLED, false);
    if (t.driverId != null) {
        requireDriverOwner(t); Driver d = driver(t.driverId);
        d.tripId = null; d.state = DriverState.AVAILABLE;
    }
    t.cancellationPaise = t.state == TripState.ARRIVED ? t.tariff.arrivedCancellationPaise() : 0;
    transition(t, TripState.CANCELLED, "rider cancellation at approved version");
    riderActive.remove(t.riderId, t.id); return new Result(Decision.CANCELLED, t.view());
}
```

This also handles acceptance racing cancellation. If cancellation wins, it retires the pending offer and releases the matching slot; acceptance then finds no current offer. If acceptance wins, the trip version changes and the old cancellation is rejected. There is no result in which T1 is cancelled while D1 remains orphaned on T1.

## 5. Complete the passenger trip and assess one fare

`ARRIVED` records the driver's report, not proof the passenger has entered. The authenticated rider's start operation moves the trip to `IN_PROGRESS`. Completion comes from the assigned driver with trusted meter evidence. The core receives actor IDs; a real API must derive and authorize those identities rather than trust arbitrary body fields.

```java
public synchronized TripView arrive(String tripId, String driverId) {
    Trip t = driverTrip(tripId, driverId);
    if (t.arrivedAt != null) return t.view();
    requireState(t, TripState.ASSIGNED); requireDriverOwner(t);
    t.arrivedAt = now(); transition(t, TripState.ARRIVED, "driver reported arrival; not proof of rider pickup");
    return t.view();
}
public synchronized TripView start(String tripId, String riderId) {
    Trip t = riderTrip(tripId, riderId);
    if (t.startedAt != null) return t.view();
    requireState(t, TripState.ARRIVED); requireDriverOwner(t);
    t.startedAt = now(); transition(t, TripState.IN_PROGRESS, "authenticated rider confirmed start");
    return t.view();
}
public synchronized TripView finish(String tripId, String driverId, int measuredMeters) {
    Trip t = driverTrip(tripId, driverId);
    if (t.completedAt != null) {
        if (!Objects.equals(t.measuredMeters, measuredMeters)) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Completion evidence conflict");
        return t.view();
    }
    if (measuredMeters < 0 || measuredMeters > 1_000_000) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Invalid meter evidence");
    requireState(t, TripState.IN_PROGRESS); requireDriverOwner(t);
    Instant now = now();
    if (now.isBefore(t.startedAt)) throw new IllegalStateException("Clock precedes trip start");
    long finalFare = fare(t.tariff, measuredMeters, Duration.between(t.startedAt, now));
    t.completedAt = now; t.measuredMeters = measuredMeters; t.finalPaise = finalFare;
    Driver d = driver(t.driverId); d.tripId = null; d.state = DriverState.AVAILABLE;
    transition(t, TripState.COMPLETED, "meter evidence frozen; cash fare due, not proof of payment");
    riderActive.remove(t.riderId, t.id); return t.view();
}
```

The example tariff is ₹40 base + ₹12 per started kilometre + ₹2 per started minute. A fixture route estimate of 2 km and five minutes gives `40 + 2×12 + 5×2 = ₹74`.

The real trip lasts eight minutes and records 2.1 km. Under this chosen started-unit policy, that is three kilometres for pricing: `40 + 3×12 + 8×2 = ₹92`. The estimate is not a fixed-price promise. The tariff is frozen on the request, so a later tariff change cannot alter this trip's calculation.

```java
public static long fare(Tariff tariff, int meters, Duration elapsed) {
    if (meters < 0 || elapsed.isNegative()) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Invalid measured usage");
    long km = meters / 1_000L + (meters % 1_000 == 0 ? 0 : 1);
    long minutes = elapsed.dividedBy(Duration.ofMinutes(1));
    if (!elapsed.minus(Duration.ofMinutes(minutes)).isZero()) minutes++;
    return Math.addExact(tariff.basePaise(), Math.addExact(
        Math.multiplyExact(km, tariff.startedKmPaise()), Math.multiplyExact(minutes, tariff.startedMinutePaise())));
}
```

The duration calculation preserves fractional minutes; truncating seconds before rounding would undercharge a minute-plus-fraction boundary. Money remains integer paise. [Java 21 Duration](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/Duration.html) supplies the duration division used here.

Completion stores the final meter/fare once and frees only this trip's driver slot. If T1's completion arrives again after D1 accepts T2, return T1's saved fare before touching current ownership. Changed meter evidence for the completed identity is a conflict, not a silent new charge.

The service records immutable event intents with `(tripId, version, state)`. Actual push messages, receipts or analytics run outside the ownership transition. A notification failure cannot undo assignment or keep a completed trip's driver busy. An invoice/fare assessment does not imply that cash or a card payment was collected.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Is the nearest driver the fastest to arrive?** Not necessarily. Road direction, traffic and access can make a farther driver faster. This solution chooses a simple geometric ranking. A production matching policy can rank fresh eligible candidates by a routing service's pickup ETA, then pass the winner through the unchanged ownership check. A real map call must happen outside the allocation transaction; its result remains a proposal that can lose the claim race.

**Why use exclusive sequential offers?** They make a driver's pending commitment unambiguous. Parallel offers may reduce rider wait, but need a first-winner transaction plus retirement of every losing offer, each guarded by its own identity. You must also decide whether one driver can consider multiple riders. This first design deliberately avoids that product/coordination complexity.

**Can GPS freshness release an accepted assignment?** No. Location says where the last observed point was; it does not say the driver stopped serving the passenger. An accepted assignment ends through a legal trip/cancellation/recovery transition. A lost phone connection needs a support/recovery policy, not a timer that makes the same driver available to another rider.

**What if the assignment response is lost?** D1 retries the same offer ID. If it was accepted, return that original trip's current view. If it expired or was cancelled, reject it. Do not create a new offer as a substitute for discovering the outcome of the old one.

**What changes when driver and trip live in separate services?** Two independent writes are not one atomic acceptance. A recoverable pending-assignment record must bind both reservations to an assignment ID, query ambiguous outcomes and publish a final assignment only when both sides agree. Compensation can release only the matching pending identity; it cannot blindly free a newer driver slot. Keeping this first core under one transaction authority avoids introducing that distributed protocol prematurely.

**Where does payment failure belong?** Physical completion and fare assessment stay recorded. A separate payment attempt uses the trip/fare identity and a stable payment key, tracks uncertainty and reconciliation, and cannot free or reassign the driver again. The cash-only mechanism does not simulate a certified payment system.

**Can we cancel after the trip starts?** Not through this ordinary pre-start operation. Completion, emergency handling, partial-trip settlement and disputes have different evidence and rules. They need explicit workflows; overwriting `IN_PROGRESS` with `CANCELLED` would lose the trip already travelled.

## 7. Closing answer

“I would use location only to propose fresh candidates, reserve one driver with an expiring offer token, and accept the driver and trip together under one authority. Old offer timers, cancellation versions and completed-trip receipts protect current ownership. Arrival and rider-confirmed start are distinct, while the frozen tariff and trusted usage determine one final fare. Push and payment work follow those facts rather than owning assignment. I would keep this transactional core intact before splitting it across services.”

## Problem

Design a food delivery system like Swiggy or Zomato: browse restaurants, place an order, the restaurant accepts and prepares it, an agent is assigned and picks it up, and the customer tracks it live. The heart of it is the **order state machine**, with **Strategy** for agent assignment and **Observer** for notifications.

## Requirements

**Functional**
- Browse restaurants and menus (with availability), build a cart from **one** restaurant, place an order, pay.
- Restaurant accepts or rejects. Agent assignment, pickup, delivery and live tracking.
- Cancellation rules that depend on the stage. Ratings for food and delivery.

**Non-functional**
- Only valid state transitions are allowed.
- An agent carries at most N orders. Assignment rules differ by city.

## 1. Scope and chosen solution

I support a single-restaurant basket, restaurant acceptance, payment, preparation and delivery. I choose an Order aggregate with immutable line/address/price snapshots; fulfilment, payment and delivery keep independent identities and state. Restaurant inventory and courier navigation are external services.

## 2. Submission and recovery contracts

```text
placeOrder(checkoutId,customer,acceptedQuote,address) -> Order
restaurantDecision(orderId,decisionId,ACCEPT|REJECT) -> CurrentState
recordPayment(orderId,providerRef,outcome) -> CurrentState
cancel(orderId,actor,expectedVersion) -> CancellationDisposition
```

Submission validates the quote and records order plus outbox event in one transaction. A relay requests payment authorization and restaurant acceptance using stable operation references. The workflow starts preparation only when its chosen acceptance/payment prerequisites hold. Duplicate callbacks are deduplicated and rejected if inconsistent with a terminal state. Kitchen acceptance after cancellation cannot restart preparation through this service.

For two ₹300 pizzas and a ₹60 drink, subtotal is ₹660; with ₹50 delivery and ₹40 discount the pre-tax total is ₹670. That quote is frozen. Restaurant rejection voids an uncollected authorization or creates a refund intent after capture. If capture outcome is unknown, reconciliation resolves the original payment before deciding which compensation applies. Courier replacement changes delivery assignment, not food-order identity. Cancellation after preparation records the applicable charge and remaining refund rather than deleting the order.

## 5. Detailed decisions, algorithms and failure behaviour

### Key abstractions

| Type | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `Restaurant`, `Menu`, `MenuItem` | Catalogue with an `isAvailable` flag |
| `Cart` → `Order` | Order snapshots item prices at placement time |
| `OrderState` | `PLACED → ACCEPTED → PREPARING → READY_FOR_PICKUP → PICKED_UP → DELIVERED`; `CANCELLED`, `REJECTED` |
| `DeliveryAgent` | Location, capacity, current orders |
| `AssignmentStrategy` | `Nearest`, `BatchingAware`, `LeastLoaded` |
| `OrderObserver` | `CustomerNotifier`, `RestaurantNotifier`, `AgentNotifier` |
| `PaymentService`, `RefundPolicy` | Refund amount by state |

```java
enum OrderStatus {
    PLACED, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY_FOR_PICKUP, PICKED_UP, DELIVERED, CANCELLED, REJECTED;
    private static final Map<OrderStatus, Set<OrderStatus>> NEXT = Map.of(
        PLACED, Set.of(ACCEPTED, REJECTED, CANCELLED),
        ACCEPTED, Set.of(PREPARING, CANCELLED), /* ... */);
    boolean canGo(OrderStatus to) { return NEXT.getOrDefault(this, Set.of()).contains(to); }
}
```

### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Transition table vs. full State classes.** A transition map on an enum is compact and easy to audit. Full State classes pay off when each state has *behaviour* (cancellation refunds, allowed edits). A common answer is the enum for legality plus a `RefundPolicy` keyed by state.

**2. When to assign the agent.** Assigning at `PLACED` wastes agent time if the restaurant is slow. Assigning at `READY` makes the customer wait. Assign when `remainingPrepTime ≈ agentTravelTime`. That's a Strategy, and it's the trade-off interviewers want discussed.

**3. Price snapshot.** Copy price and name into `OrderItem`. Menu edits after ordering must not change the bill.

**4. Observer for fan-out.** Each transition publishes `OrderStatusChanged`. Notifiers, ETA recompute and analytics subscribe independently.

**5. Concurrency.** The restaurant's accept and the customer's cancel can race. Guard transitions with a conditional update (`compareAndSet` on status, or `UPDATE … WHERE status = :expected`), so only one wins.

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** An order coordinates restaurant, courier and payment states without pretending they are one atomic action.

Restaurant owns menu versions; Cart holds selections; Order snapshots item names, options, prices and delivery address. RestaurantOrder records acceptance and preparation; Delivery records assignment and proof of delivery. FeePolicy and PaymentGateway are separate ports.

**Correctness rule:** An accepted order retains the agreed price and items even when the live menu changes. A delivered order must not return to preparing because a late event arrives.

```text
orders(id,menu_version,total_minor,state,version); order_line(snapshot); delivery(order_id,courier_id,status); payment_attempt(id,status)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Validate menu and service area; create price snapshot; authorize payment; ask restaurant to accept; capture according to policy; assign a courier; record pickup and delivery; handle cancellation/refund through explicit compensating operations.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

A restaurant accepts while the customer cancels. Compare the order version and use cancellation rules for the resulting state. Deduplicate provider and courier events by event identity, not by matching message text.

### Policy choices and extension seams

Separate payment, preparation and delivery state axes avoid one enormous enum with impossible combinations. A coordinator keeps legal combinations explicit. Observer-style notifications may update views but must not commit payment state.

### Edge cases that change the design

One unavailable menu item may require partial acceptance with customer approval. An address change after courier assignment affects fees and serviceability. No courier available is a recoverable dispatch problem, not evidence that food was never prepared.

**Topic-specific cases:** Menu price changes do not alter an order; duplicate pickup ignored; cancel/accept race; partial item rejection; payment uncertainty; invalid address change.

### Further reading

- [Java 21 concurrency contracts](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/util/concurrent/package-summary.html) — synchronization and executor semantics.
- [PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html) — database guarantees differ from process locks.

### Work through retries and cancellation

The same restaurant acceptance event may arrive twice. Apply it once by decision identity and legal source state. An acceptance arriving after cancellation must not resurrect the order. Similarly, reassigning a courier should not create a second restaurant preparation request.

Cancellation before preparation may be free; cancellation after food is prepared may have a charge. Put this rule in a cancellation policy using recorded milestones. Do not inspect a stale UI label and guess whether the kitchen has started.

Keep network calls outside database locks. Record intent, call the provider, then record the result. If the process stops between those steps, resume using the original operation reference. A void and a refund are different actions depending on whether money was only authorized or already captured.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Why snapshot the address?** Editing a profile after dispatch must not redirect an already accepted delivery without an explicit change operation.

**Does a payment success mean restaurant acceptance?** No. Financial and fulfilment outcomes are separate.

## 7. Closing answer

My order freezes accepted commercial terms and coordinates separate restaurant, financial and delivery states. Recorded milestones decide cancellation and compensation. Idempotent events advance only legal transitions, so duplicate acceptance or courier events cannot resurrect a cancelled order or repeat payment.

## Problem

Design a library system that can find books, lend physical copies, accept returns, renew loans and keep a fair waiting list.

Suppose A borrows the only copy of *Practical Java*. B and then C ask to borrow it next. When A returns it, the system must offer the copy to B. If B misses the pickup deadline, C gets a new assignment. A delayed timer for B must not take the book away from C.

That small story gives us the central distinction: the catalogue describes a book edition; circulation tracks who currently owns the right to a particular physical copy.

## Requirements

I will choose these policies for one branch:

- Search edition metadata by title, author or ISBN. A physical copy has its own barcode.
- A member has an immutable membership plan. Basic allows two active loans, a 14-day loan, one renewal and a 50-cent fine per overdue day. Plus allows four active loans, 21 days, one renewal and 25 cents per day. These are example policies, not universal library rules.
- Only active members may borrow, renew or place a hold. They must be below their loan limit to borrow or join the queue, and may borrow only one copy of an edition at a time.
- Holds are FIFO across copies of one edition at this branch. We do not substitute a different edition. Assign a healthy returned copy directly to the oldest eligible waiting reader.
- An assignment made on date D is valid through `D + 2 calendar days`, inclusive. After that date it may expire and the next reader gets a new pickup token.
- A loan is due through the end of its local due date. Renewal adds the original loan period to the existing due date, and is denied after it is overdue, after its renewal allowance is used or while another reader has an outstanding hold.
- Returning a book closes that loan and records any fine once. An inactive member may still return. Unpaid fines do not block circulation under this simplified policy, and the return never waits for a payment network.
- A damaged return goes to repair. It cannot be lent or offered for pickup until repair is completed.

The runnable example is an in-memory Java application with an injected branch clock. Authentication, payment collection, real messages, durable storage, lost-item billing and other branches are adapter or extension work; the precise transaction and recovery boundaries are explained below.

## 1. Begin with one title and one barcode

`BookEdition E1` describes *Practical Java*: its title, author and ISBN. `Copy C17` identifies the item on a shelf. If the library buys another identical copy, it creates another barcode, not another edition.

A loan needs both the copy and the borrower. **L1** means “A's borrowing visit using C17.” Later, C can borrow C17 under **L2**. The barcode stays the same; the loan identity changes.

| Object | Example | Why it is separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BookEdition` | E1, *Practical Java* | Catalogue metadata is shared by all copies of this edition. |
| `Copy` | Barcode C17 | Availability, damage and current ownership belong to the physical item. |
| `Member` with `Plan` | A has Basic | Policy values do not require a subclass per membership tier. |
| `Loan` | L1: A borrowed C17 | Due dates, renewals, return and fine belong to one borrowing visit. |
| `Hold` | H-B: B waits for E1 | B wants the next suitable copy, not a permanent claim to C17. |
| `Pickup` | P1: C17 is reserved for B | A specific assignment needs its own token and deadline. |

The important rule is **one active loan per copy**, not one loan per title. Two copies of E1 can be on loan to different members at the same time. Our additional one-copy-per-edition-per-member rule prevents the same member taking both.

## 2. Keep one authority for circulation

`Service` owns the editions, copies, members, loans, hold queues and command receipts. The small implementation synchronizes circulation operations on this service. A desk or browser asks it to perform a command; it cannot edit a `Copy` array or keep its own authoritative free-copy list.

These are the public values used by the implementation:

```java
public enum CopyState { AVAILABLE, ON_LOAN, PICKUP, REPAIR }
public enum HoldState { WAITING, PICKUP, FULFILLED, EXPIRED, CANCELLED }
public enum Condition { GOOD, DAMAGED }
public record BookEdition(String id, String isbn, String title, String author) {
    public BookEdition { key(id); key(isbn); key(title); key(author); }
}
public record Plan(String name, int maxLoans, int loanDays, int maxRenewals, long fineCentsPerDay) {
    public Plan {
        key(name);
        if (maxLoans < 1 || loanDays < 1 || maxRenewals < 0 || fineCentsPerDay < 0)
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Invalid plan");
    }
}
public record Pickup(String token, String holdId, String memberId, LocalDate pickupBy) {}
public record CopyView(String id, String editionId, CopyState state, String loanId, Pickup pickup) {}
public record MemberView(String id, boolean active, Plan plan, long activeLoans) {}
public record LoanView(String id, String copyId, String memberId, LocalDate checkedOut,
                       LocalDate due, LocalDate returned, int renewals, Plan terms) {}

                       Pickup pickup, String reason) {}

```

`Member` composes a `Plan`. The loan retains the plan values it received at checkout, so a future policy change need not recalculate yesterday's due date or fine rate. In this example plans and published views are immutable, and no plan-change operation is offered.

The system preserves these facts after every completed operation:

- An `ON_LOAN` copy refers to exactly one open loan; an open loan belongs to exactly one copy and member.
- Member limits are checked together with copy ownership, so two desks cannot each consume the same last borrowing slot.
- A `PICKUP` copy has one current member, token and deadline. It is unavailable to everyone else.
- The edition queue assigns readers in arrival order among those still eligible. At allocation, an inactive member, a member now at their loan limit or someone already borrowing that edition is cancelled with a recorded reason; the next eligible reader is considered. Rejoining later requires a new hold.
- A return receipt is identified by its loan. Replaying it cannot reassess the fine or modify a later loan of the same barcode.
- Fine assessment and ownership changes contain no payment or notification network calls.

A pickup reservation does not reserve a borrowing slot. Eligibility is checked again at checkout. A member who fills their limit with other books must return one before collecting the pickup, or allow its deadline to expire. This is a deliberately simple policy with an explicit consequence.

## 3. Checkout and renewal protect both member and copy

Checkout takes a stable request ID, a barcode, a member ID and, for an assigned copy, the current pickup token.

It first returns any matching saved checkout receipt. A reused request ID with a different payload is rejected. For a new request, it checks membership, current loan count and the one-copy-per-edition rule; then it requires an available copy or an unexpired pickup assignment belonging to this member and token. Finally it creates a loan and changes the copy to `ON_LOAN` together.

```java
public synchronized LoanView checkout(String requestId, String copyId, String memberId, String pickupToken) {
    key(requestId);
    CheckoutCommand previous = checkoutCommands.get(requestId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!Objects.equals(copyId,previous.copyId()) || !Objects.equals(memberId,previous.memberId())
            || !Objects.equals(pickupToken,previous.pickupToken())) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Checkout ID payload conflict");
        return previous.receipt();
    }
    Copy copy = item(copyId); Member member = member(memberId); LocalDate today = today();
    requireEligible(member,copy.editionId);
    if (copy.state == CopyState.PICKUP) {
        if (!copy.pickup.memberId().equals(memberId) || !copy.pickup.token().equals(pickupToken)
            || today.isAfter(copy.pickup.pickupBy())) throw new IllegalStateException("Pickup does not belong to this request or has expired");
    } else if (copy.state != CopyState.AVAILABLE || pickupToken != null) {
        throw new IllegalStateException("Copy unavailable or stale pickup token");
    }
    Loan loan = new Loan("L" + ++loanSequence,copy,member,today);
    if (copy.pickup != null) holdRecord(copy.pickup.holdId()).state = HoldState.FULFILLED;
    copy.pickup = null; copy.state = CopyState.ON_LOAN; copy.loanId = loan.id;
    loans.put(loan.id,loan);
    LoanView receipt = loan.view();
    checkoutCommands.put(requestId,new CheckoutCommand(copyId,memberId,pickupToken,receipt));
    return receipt;
}
```

Choosing C17 in search does not reserve it. Two desks may see it in a result, but only one authoritative checkout can change it to `ON_LOAN`. The other desk must read the updated state. The same lock also protects the member limit when the desks choose different copies.

The checkout receipt records the original checkout response. After renewal or return, use `loan(id)` to read the current loan, rather than treating a replayed old receipt as a new observation.

### A renewal is an extension of one loan

For a Basic loan issued on October 1, the due date is October 15. Renewing on October 12 extends the existing due date by 14 days to October 29. It does not create another loan or count as another borrowed item.

Renewal checks the actual loan, borrower, active membership, current date, saved renewal allowance and other readers' outstanding holds. A waiting hold and a ready pickup assignment both count as demand. The request ID prevents a lost response from extending the due date twice.

```java
public synchronized RenewalReceipt renew(String requestId, String loanId, String memberId) {
    key(requestId);
    RenewalCommand previous = renewalCommands.get(requestId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!Objects.equals(loanId,previous.loanId()) || !Objects.equals(memberId,previous.memberId()))
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Renewal ID payload conflict");
        return previous.receipt();
    }
    Loan loan = loanRecord(loanId); Member member = member(memberId);
    if (!loan.memberId.equals(memberId) || loan.receipt != null || !member.active
        || today().isAfter(loan.due) || loan.renewals >= loan.terms.maxRenewals())
        throw new IllegalStateException("Loan cannot be renewed");
    if (holds.values().stream().anyMatch(h -> h.editionId.equals(item(loan.copyId).editionId)
        && !h.memberId.equals(memberId) && (h.state == HoldState.WAITING || h.state == HoldState.PICKUP)))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Another reader has an outstanding hold");
    loan.due = loan.due.plusDays(loan.terms.loanDays()); loan.renewals++;
    RenewalReceipt receipt = new RenewalReceipt(loanId,loan.due,loan.renewals);
    renewalCommands.put(requestId,new RenewalCommand(loanId,memberId,receipt));
    return receipt;
}
```

We keep the policy conservative: an outstanding hold blocks renewal until that hold is fulfilled, expires or is cancelled during allocation. Renewal does not silently delete someone else's request to make itself succeed.

## 4. Return the item, assess the fine and serve the queue

A return targets **barcode plus loan ID**. Closing every loan for E1 would affect other copies; closing “whatever loan currently uses C17” would make a delayed event dangerous.

The return operation does this under the circulation lock:

1. Find that loan and verify its barcode.
2. If it already has a return receipt, return the saved receipt before touching current copy ownership. A changed condition under the same return token is rejected.
3. Require C17's active loan to still be that loan.
4. Compute overdue days and save the one return/fine assessment.
5. Close the loan and move a good copy to allocation, or a damaged copy to `REPAIR`.
6. Assign a healthy copy directly to the oldest eligible hold, or leave it available if nobody is waiting. Record a pickup-notice intent.

```java
public synchronized ReturnReceipt returnCopy(String copyId, String expectedLoanId, Condition condition) {
    Objects.requireNonNull(condition);
    Loan loan = loanRecord(expectedLoanId);
    if (!loan.copyId.equals(copyId)) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Loan belongs to another copy");
    if (loan.receipt != null) {
        if (loan.receipt.condition() != condition) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Return token payload conflict");
        return loan.receipt; // Old return replay cannot touch the copy's current owner.
    }
    Copy copy = item(copyId);
    if (copy.state != CopyState.ON_LOAN || !expectedLoanId.equals(copy.loanId))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Stale loan token");
    LocalDate today = today();
    if (today.isBefore(loan.checkedOut)) throw new IllegalStateException("Return date precedes checkout");
    long overdueDays = Math.max(0,ChronoUnit.DAYS.between(loan.due,today));
    long fine = Math.multiplyExact(overdueDays,loan.terms.fineCentsPerDay());
    loan.receipt = new ReturnReceipt(loan.id,today,condition,overdueDays,fine);
    copy.loanId = null; copy.state = condition == Condition.DAMAGED ? CopyState.REPAIR : CopyState.AVAILABLE;
    allocate(copy.editionId,today); // Record ownership and ready notices, never call a payment network.
    return loan.receipt;
}
```

![Returning C17 closes the specific loan, records its fine once and sends a healthy copy directly to the first eligible edition hold. A damaged copy goes to repair; payment happens separately.](learning-figures/lld-9-return.svg)

There is no call to `collectPayment` here. A payment service can fail or take seconds; the library should still accept its physical book and let the next reader use it. The fine is an amount owed, keyed by the loan ID, not proof of payment. A separate payment adapter can collect against that charge with its own stable payment ID and receipt.

### Calculate calendar days explicitly

Configure the branch clock with its time zone; the example uses `Asia/Kolkata`. [`LocalDate`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/LocalDate.html) is a calendar date without a time or zone, so the service obtains today's date from that configured clock. It does not divide elapsed milliseconds by 86,400,000.

For A's Basic loan:

```text
Checkout:        2026-10-01
Due date:        2026-10-01 + 14 calendar days = 2026-10-15
Returned:        2026-10-18
Overdue days:    max(0, DAYS.between(2026-10-15, 2026-10-18)) = 3
Fine:            3 × 50 cents = 150 cents = $1.50
```

The 15th is included as an on-time date. Return on the 15th costs zero, on the 16th costs 50 cents and on the 18th costs $1.50. Java's [`ChronoUnit.between`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/temporal/ChronoUnit.html#between(java.time.temporal.Temporal,java.time.temporal.Temporal)) supplies the date difference. We count every calendar date, with no holiday adjustment, grace period or cap in this chosen policy.

Replaying L1's return on November 1 still returns the saved $1.50 receipt. It does not recalculate a larger fine using the retry date.

### FIFO holds need assignment tokens

The hold queue is keyed by edition E1. `allocate` removes the oldest eligible hold, then creates a fresh pickup assignment for a specific copy. A notice is only a stored intent here; actual messaging happens outside the circulation operation.

```java
private void allocate(String editionId, LocalDate today) {
    ArrayDeque<Hold> queue = waiting.get(editionId);
    if (queue == null) return;
    List<Copy> available = copies.values().stream().filter(c -> c.editionId.equals(editionId)
        && c.state == CopyState.AVAILABLE).sorted(Comparator.comparing(c -> c.id)).toList();
    for (Copy copy : available) {
        while (!queue.isEmpty()) {
            Hold hold = queue.removeFirst(); Member member = member(hold.memberId);
            String reason = ineligibility(member,editionId);
            if (reason != null) { hold.state = HoldState.CANCELLED; hold.reason = reason; continue; }
            Pickup pickup = new Pickup("P" + ++pickupSequence,hold.id,hold.memberId,today.plusDays(2));
            hold.state = HoldState.PICKUP; hold.pickup = pickup;
            copy.state = CopyState.PICKUP; copy.pickup = pickup;
            notices.add(new PickupNotice(pickup.token(),member.id,copy.id,pickup.pickupBy()));
            break;
        }
    }
}
```

Our example is now:

| Date/event | C17 ownership | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| October 1: A checks out | Loan L1, member A | Due October 15. |
| October 2: B then C queue | Still L1 | Waiting does not end A's loan. |
| October 18: A returns | Pickup P1, member B | B is first; pickup through October 20. Fine $1.50 is recorded once. |
| October 20: expiry worker runs | Still P1 | The deadline date is inclusive. |
| October 21: P1 expires | Pickup P2, member C | C gets a fresh assignment, through October 23. |
| Delayed P1 timer arrives | Still P2 | P1 no longer owns this assignment. |
| C collects with P2 | Loan L2, member C | Plus gives a 21-day loan, due November 11. |
| Delayed return for L1 arrives | Still L2 | Replay L1's saved receipt; do not free C's copy. |

![Pickup token P1 can expire only its own assignment. After C gets P2 or loan L2, an old P1 timer and an old L1 return cannot release C17.](learning-figures/lld-9-tokens.svg)

These are the complete hold creation and expiry operations:

```java
public synchronized HoldView placeHold(String holdId, String editionId, String memberId) {
    key(holdId); edition(editionId); Member member = member(memberId);
    Hold previous = holds.get(holdId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!previous.editionId.equals(editionId) || !previous.memberId.equals(memberId))
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Hold ID payload conflict");
        return previous.view();
    }
    requireEligible(member,editionId);
    if (holds.values().stream().anyMatch(h -> h.editionId.equals(editionId) && h.memberId.equals(memberId)
        && (h.state == HoldState.WAITING || h.state == HoldState.PICKUP)))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Member already has an outstanding hold for this edition");
    Hold hold = new Hold(holdId,editionId,memberId);
    holds.put(holdId,hold); waiting.computeIfAbsent(editionId,ignored -> new ArrayDeque<>()).addLast(hold);
    allocate(editionId,today());
    return hold.view();
}

public synchronized boolean expirePickup(String copyId, String expectedPickupToken) {
    Copy copy = item(copyId);
    if (copy.state != CopyState.PICKUP || !copy.pickup.token().equals(expectedPickupToken)
        || !today().isAfter(copy.pickup.pickupBy())) return false;
    holdRecord(copy.pickup.holdId()).state = HoldState.EXPIRED;
    copy.pickup = null; copy.state = CopyState.AVAILABLE;
    allocate(copy.editionId,today());
    return true;
}
```

Notice the order in `expirePickup`: check copy state and **current token** before looking at the deadline or changing anything. An old timer does not get to release whichever reservation happens to be there now. The same pattern protects a consumed pickup after checkout.

The scheduler must eventually call expiry for overdue assignments. Checkout itself rejects an expired pickup; it does not let another reader take it before the queue has been advanced. Delayed job execution can reduce availability temporarily, but cannot transfer ownership incorrectly.

### What changes when there is a database?

The in-memory lock protects callers sharing one service object, and the records disappear on restart. A durable adapter must commit the active loan, copy state, member capacity check, affected edition queue, return/fine receipt and notice intent as one circulation transition. Store command IDs and tokens with those records so a lost response or restarted worker can identify the original operation.

For this small single branch, serializing all circulation writes behind one database transaction boundary is a valid simple starting point. If later using finer locks, every writer must coordinate the member limit, copy and edition allocation consistently. Locking only the copy does not protect a member's last slot across two different copies. Locking only the loan does not protect renewal against a newly placed hold.

A database can also reject duplicate active loans or fine identities using its supported constraints/indexes, but the syntax and guarantees depend on the chosen database and transaction design. An ordinary `UNIQUE(copy_id)` over all loan history is wrong: it would prevent that copy ever being borrowed again. Do not infer complete circulation correctness from the phrase “use SQL transactions.”

Catalogue search can later become a separate indexed projection. Search may be stale; checkout must still consult the authoritative circulation state. This implementation uses a simple case-insensitive scan and makes no million-title latency claim.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Is the hold really title-level if its key is an edition ID?** It is above the copy level: any healthy copy of E1 can satisfy it. We deliberately treat one edition as the requested catalogue title and do not substitute a translation or later edition. If the product groups editions into a work, add a `Work` identity and define which editions satisfy that queue; do not silently use ISBN as both a work and a barcode.

**Why not subclass Member into Student and Faculty?** The behavior is the same; only values differ. A member has a Plan containing limits, duration, renewals and fine rate. Composition makes a new tier another value configuration. A separate policy interface becomes useful only when the rules themselves become different algorithms.

**What if the oldest waiting member becomes inactive or reaches their limit?** At allocation, cancel that hold with the reason and continue in FIFO order among eligible readers. This selected policy avoids leaving a healthy copy idle behind an ineligible request. A product could instead suspend the hold, but it would need a precise rule for when it re-enters the queue.

**May a ready notice free or transfer a copy?** No. The notice carries its pickup token and deadline. A sender can suppress an obsolete notice by checking the current assignment, but delivery success is not ownership. Only the circulation operation may expire or consume that matching token. A durable notice adapter should deduplicate by pickup token and retain an outbox record until handled.

**Can an old return clear the current borrower's loan?** Returning by barcode alone could do that. Here the loan ID is the return token. A completed old loan returns its saved receipt before any current copy mutation. A token for another barcode is rejected. Staff can display current ownership separately if an old receipt is replayed.

**Do overdue fines have to be paid before accepting a return?** No under our policy. Accepting the item, closing the loan and assessing the debt form one local circulation operation. Payment collection is separate and cannot prevent inventory from being returned. If borrowing eligibility later depends on unpaid fines, specify that policy and coordinate its authoritative debt state; do not add a slow payment call inside return.

**What about a book returned damaged or reported lost?** Damage is implemented: the loan closes, but the copy enters REPAIR and waiting readers remain queued until a healthy copy is available. Lost-item handling is outside scope; it needs its own inventory state and replacement-charge policy. Closing a loan alone must never imply that a missing copy is on the shelf.

## 7. Closing answer

“I would separate edition metadata, physical copies and borrowing visits. One circulation boundary checks member limits and changes copy ownership, while an edition FIFO queue assigns returned healthy copies with expiring pickup tokens. Returns target a loan ID, record the date-based fine once and move inventory without waiting on payment. Renewal respects other readers and reuses a command receipt on retry. The in-memory example demonstrates those rules; a durable adapter must preserve the same transition and token checks across commits and restarts.”

## Problem

Design a shared-expense application such as Splitwise. People record what they paid, divide the cost and see how to settle up. The answer must keep every paise accounted for, handle repeated requests and explain what happens when an old expense is corrected after somebody has already paid.

## Requirements

**Chosen scope:** one group has a fixed set of members and uses INR. An expense can have one or several payers. It supports equal, exact, percentage and weighted-share allocation. Members see the group's expenses and balances; the application can suggest a simpler payment route. A sender reports a payment and its receiver confirms receipt. Corrections append reversals instead of silently changing history.

This is an expense tracker. It does **not** initiate a bank transfer or verify what happened in a bank account. Expense records are statements made by group members. Payment confirmation records agreement between the two people; neither is a bank reconciliation service.

**Correctness requirements:** shares and payer contributions each add to the expense total; signed group balances always add to zero; a repeated operation with the same identity changes nothing twice; changed data under an existing identity is rejected; a reader never sees half of a journal update. The complete Java example demonstrates these rules inside one process. Database durability is a separate boundary explained below.

## 1. Begin with a trip, not a design-pattern name

Asha, Ben and Cara take a taxi. Asha pays **₹100.01**, shared equally. We store that amount as **10,001 paise**, an integer. Dividing it into three independent rounded decimals would make it easy to lose or create a paise.

Start with 3,333 paise each. That accounts for 9,999 paise, so two paise remain. Our documented tie rule gives an extra paise to the first two member IDs in sorted order: Asha, then Ben.

| Member | Paid | Own share | Net balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asha | ₹100.01 | ₹33.34 | +₹66.67 |
| Ben | ₹0 | ₹33.34 | −₹33.34 |
| Cara | ₹0 | ₹33.33 | −₹33.33 |

**Positive means “is owed”; negative means “owes.”** Asha does not owe herself ₹33.34. Her contribution and her own share cancel inside her balance: `10001 − 3334 = 6667` paise. The group adds to zero: `6667 − 3334 − 3333 = 0`.

Ben then buys ₹30 of snacks for Ben and Cara. Each owes ₹15 of that expense. Ben paid ₹30 and owes ₹15, so his balance rises by ₹15; Cara's falls by ₹15. Asha is not a participant in the snack expense and her balance does not change.

The new balances are Asha **+₹66.67**, Ben **−₹18.34**, Cara **−₹48.33**. Ben can pay Asha ₹18.34 and Cara can pay Asha ₹48.33. These amounts settle the group's net positions.

[Step through the trip, retries and a later correction](learning-labs/splitwise.html). Its frames come from the Java ledger below; no money is sent.

Before choosing the classes, I would clarify four product rules:

- **Can group debts be rerouted?** In this design, members accept net settlement within their group. Ben may be asked to pay Cara even if the original expense was paid by Asha. If the product promises original-creditor-only debts, keep the pairwise obligations and constrain suggestions instead.
- **Which currency?** One fixed currency per ledger. Adding INR and USD balances is meaningless without an explicit conversion transaction and recorded rate.
- **Who can record or correct an expense?** Any authenticated member can record it for the group. Only its original recorder can reverse it in this version. Other members dispute it through a separate workflow; they cannot silently rewrite it.
- **What does “paid” mean?** A sender's report is pending. Only the receiver's authenticated confirmation changes the ledger. This is a chosen collaboration rule, not proof from a payment provider.

## 2. Keep allocation, recorded facts and suggestions separate

[![The group ledger records expense snapshots; allocation computes exact shares; expenses and confirmed payments post balanced journal entries; the journal rebuilds balances; suggestions are derived from balances.](designs/lld-10.svg)](designs/lld-10.svg)

These are objects inside one application, not seven services.

| Type | One clear job |
| --- | --- |
| `Splits` | Turn a split rule into exact nonnegative paise per participant. It never changes a balance. |
| `Expense` | Keep the immutable original payer contributions, total, participant shares and recorder. |
| `SplitLedger` | Own one group's authorization checks, operation identities, journal and balance changes. |
| `Entry` | Record an immutable balanced change caused by an expense, confirmed payment or reversal. |
| `Payment` | Keep sender, receiver, amount and `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` state. |
| `Snapshot` / `Plan` | Return immutable views. A plan is advice based on one revision; it reserves nothing. |

The central formula is the same for every expense:

`change for a member = amount that member paid − amount that member owes`

With multiple payers, the formula still works. If Asha pays ₹6 and Ben pays ₹4 for a ₹10 purchase, their contributions sum to ₹10. Allocate the ₹10 among the chosen participants, then subtract each person's share from their contribution. We do not need a separate ledger algorithm for multiple payers.

The split-policy seam follows the **single-responsibility principle**: allocation decides shares; posting decides how recorded facts affect balances. Equal, percentage and weighted methods share a small allocation algorithm. There is no benefit in creating a class for every individual participant, or a factory hierarchy just to return four stateless functions. An API adapter can select the appropriate allocation function from a split type; the ledger accepts only the validated final shares.

The intended API boundary is small:

| Operation | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Add expense | Group, stable expense ID, authenticated recorder, total, payer contributions and split instruction. Server calculates/validates final shares. Same ID plus same recorded facts returns the original result; changed facts conflict. |
| Read group balance/history | Authenticated current member only. Return one immutable snapshot/revision including original expense facts, payment reports and reversal-to-expense links. |
| Report a payment | Stable payment ID, authenticated sender, receiver and positive amount. Record a proposal without changing balances. |
| Confirm receipt | Authenticated receiver confirms that payment ID. Post it once. The sender cannot confirm on the receiver's behalf. |
| Correct an expense | Original recorder supplies a stable reversal ID. Append the exact opposite of that expense once; add a replacement as a new expense if needed. |
| Suggest settlement | Return proposed transfers and their snapshot revision. No balance or payment changes. |

IDs are scoped by group and operation kind: an expense ID and a payment ID are different namespaces. Authentication supplies the actor from a trusted session; accepting an arbitrary `actor` field from a browser would defeat the authorization checks. The Java core accepts that already authenticated identity. It is not an HTTP/authentication implementation.

## 3. Split the total without losing a paise

The complete `Splits` class supports all four rules:

| Rule | Example | Validation and result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equal | ₹100.01 among A, B, C | 3334 / 3334 / 3333 paise; stable member-ID tie order |
| Exact | ₹10 with A owing ₹2 and B owing ₹8 | Nonnegative shares must total exactly 1000 paise |
| Percentage | ₹1.01 at 50% / 30% / 20% | 51 / 30 / 20 paise after remainder allocation; percentages total 100% |
| Weighted shares | ₹1.01 at A:B = 1:2 | A owes 34 paise and B owes 67 paise |

Percentages use **basis points**: 100% is 10,000 units, so 12.50% is 1,250. This example supports hundredths of a percentage point. A product needing finer proportions must choose a different documented scale; it must not silently round the percentages first.

For weighted allocation, first compute every participant's exact proportional amount. Keep its whole-paise part and fractional remainder. Give the unallocated paise to the largest remainders, breaking equal remainders by member ID. That is why the ₹1.01 ratio 1:2 gives 34/67 rather than truncating to 33/67 and losing one paise.

```java
private static Map<String, Long> allocate(long total, Map<String, Integer> weights, boolean allowZero) {
    if (total <= 0 || weights.isEmpty()) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Positive total and weights required");
    BigInteger denominator = BigInteger.ZERO;
    for (var e : weights.entrySet()) {
        validId(e.getKey());
        if (e.getValue() == null || e.getValue() < 0 || (!allowZero && e.getValue() == 0))
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Invalid weight");
        denominator = denominator.add(BigInteger.valueOf(e.getValue()));
    }
    if (denominator.signum() == 0) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Zero total weight");
    var shares = new TreeMap<String, Long>();
    var fractions = new HashMap<String, BigInteger>();
    long used = 0;
    for (var e : weights.entrySet()) {
        BigInteger numerator = BigInteger.valueOf(total).multiply(BigInteger.valueOf(e.getValue()));
        BigInteger[] division = numerator.divideAndRemainder(denominator);
        long floor = division[0].longValueExact();
        shares.put(e.getKey(), floor); fractions.put(e.getKey(), division[1]);
        used = Math.addExact(used, floor);
    }
    var order = new ArrayList<>(shares.keySet());
    order.sort(Comparator.<String, BigInteger>comparing(fractions::get).reversed().thenComparing(id -> id));
    int remaining = Math.toIntExact(total - used); // Less than the number of participants.
    for (int i = 0; i < remaining; i++) shares.compute(order.get(i), (id, amount) -> amount + 1);
    return Collections.unmodifiableMap(shares);
}
```

`BigInteger` is used only for the intermediate multiplication and division. A valid total can fit in `long` while `total × weight` does not. The resulting share is still an integer `long` paise amount. Exact allocation verifies the total with the same care. Zero shares are allowed, but the expense total is positive and at least one participant exists. Negative adjustments use the explicit reversal operation.

This tie rule is predictable, not a promise that every person receives an equal number of extra paise over their lifetime. Display the final shares before saving. Changing the rounding policy later must not recalculate historical expenses: their already agreed shares are snapshots.

## 4. Post one whole fact, then return a snapshot

Suppose two requests try to record the taxi expense with ID `taxi-1`. Checking the ID outside the update lock is insufficient: both could see it missing and both post the money.

The ledger checks identity and updates the journal/projection inside one short synchronized method. It compares the complete normalized expense, including its recorder, payer map and final shares. Map order does not change those facts.

```java
public synchronized Expense addExpense(String id, String actor, long total,
                                        Map<String, Long> paid, Map<String, Long> shares) {
    member(actor); Splits.validId(id);
    Map<String, Long> checkedPaid = Splits.exact(total, paid);
    Map<String, Long> checkedShares = Splits.exact(total, shares);
    checkedPaid.keySet().forEach(this::member); checkedShares.keySet().forEach(this::member);
    Expense input = new Expense(id, actor, total, checkedPaid, checkedShares);
    Expense old = expenses.get(id);
    if (old != null) {
        if (!old.equals(input)) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Expense identity reused with changed data");
        return old;
    }
    var deltas = new TreeMap<String, Long>();
    for (String user : members)
        deltas.put(user, checkedPaid.getOrDefault(user, 0L) - checkedShares.getOrDefault(user, 0L));
    post(new Entry("EXPENSE", id, deltas));
    expenses.put(id, input);
    return input;
}
```

The first request records the expense. A retry returns that immutable expense. Reusing its ID for ₹100.02 raises a conflict and changes nothing. Retrying the original expense after it was later reversed also returns its original snapshot; a retry must not resurrect its financial effect.

For each new journal entry, calculate **all** candidate balances before committing any of them:

```java
private void post(Entry entry) {
    var candidate = new TreeMap<>(balances);
    for (String user : members) {
        long value = Math.addExact(candidate.get(user), entry.deltas().getOrDefault(user, 0L));
        Math.negateExact(value); // Every committed balance must have a representable magnitude.
        candidate.put(user, value);
    }
    long next = Math.incrementExact(revision);
    // All fallible arithmetic is complete before either journal or projection changes.
    entries.add(entry); balances = candidate; revision = next;
}
```

Java's [`Math.addExact`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/lang/Math.html) detects integer overflow rather than wrapping a large credit into a debt. The mechanism also requires every committed magnitude to be representable, so it can safely negate a debtor balance when planning. An unsupported amount fails before the journal, projection or operation result changes. A real payment adapter must retain unresolved receipt evidence when a supported storage limit prevents recording it; it must not acknowledge and discard an actual transfer.

Every public read uses the same aggregate boundary and returns copies with immutable members/entries. Readers cannot mutate the live maps. [`Map.copyOf`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/util/Map.html) makes the map unmodifiable, but does not promise sorted iteration; the trace explicitly sorts IDs for display.

The member snapshot also includes original expenses and each reversal’s original expense ID; the same net deltas cannot reconstruct who paid which total or how it was split. The journal is the explanation of the balance, not an optional debugging log. Replaying its entries in recorded order reconstructs the same projection. In this local example both live only in memory, so restarting the JVM loses them.

**With a database**, create the group row first. For a small group, lock that existing group row while checking the operation identity and writing its expense/payment/reversal, journal entries and balance projection in one transaction. A unique `(group_id, operation_kind, operation_id)` backs up the identity rule. Do not commit the journal but update the projection in a later unrelated transaction. A lost response is retried with the same ID and original data. This is a concrete adapter design, not a database integration claimed by the in-process test.

An extremely active group may need a different concurrency strategy. A versioned group snapshot with bounded whole-transaction retry is one option; deterministic ordering is needed if an operation locks multiple member rows. Begin with the simple group boundary until measured contention justifies changing it.

## 5. A suggestion, a payment report and confirmed receipt are different things

![Ben reports a payment, it waits with balances unchanged, and Asha's confirmation posts one balanced change. Repeating the confirmation creates no second entry.](learning-figures/lld-10-payment.svg)

Ben reports sending Asha ₹18.34. The record is `PROPOSED`. His debt still appears until Asha confirms receipt. On confirmation, Ben's balance rises by 1,834 paise and Asha's falls by 1,834. Both changes happen together and their sum is zero.

```java
public synchronized Payment proposePayment(String id, String actor, String to, long amount) {
    member(actor); member(to); Splits.validId(id);
    if (actor.equals(to) || amount <= 0) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Distinct parties and positive amount required");
    Payment old = payments.get(id);
    if (old != null) {
        if (!old.from().equals(actor) || !old.to().equals(to) || old.amount() != amount)
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Payment identity reused with changed data");
        return old;
    }
    long next = Math.incrementExact(revision);
    Payment result = new Payment(id, actor, to, amount, PaymentState.PROPOSED);
    payments.put(id, result); revision = next; // Proposal changes no balance.
    return result;
}

public synchronized Payment confirmPayment(String id, String actor) {
    member(actor);
    Payment old = payments.get(id);
    if (old == null) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Unknown payment");
    if (!old.to().equals(actor)) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Only the receiver confirms receipt");
    if (old.state() == PaymentState.CONFIRMED) return old;
    // Record the confirmed real-world payment, even if a newer expense changed today's debt.
    post(new Entry("PAYMENT", id, Map.of(old.from(), old.amount(), old.to(), -old.amount())));
    Payment result = new Payment(id, old.from(), old.to(), old.amount(), PaymentState.CONFIRMED);
    payments.put(id, result);
    return result;
}
```

The payment's amount and parties cannot change under its ID. Repeated confirmation returns `CONFIRMED` without another journal entry. A receiver who disagrees simply does not confirm; dispute/cancellation policy is outside this two-state receipt mechanism. Production would retain that pending report and its evidence until resolved, rather than silently treating it as a successful settlement.

There is a subtle race here: **do not reject an actual received payment merely because today's suggested debt changed.**

After the taxi, Ben owes ₹33.34. He reports paying that amount. Before Asha confirms, Ben records ₹12 of tickets bought solely for Asha. Now Ben owes only ₹21.34. If Asha really received ₹33.34, record the full confirmed transfer: Ben becomes a creditor of ₹12. Rewriting it to ₹21.34 would lose the fact that money changed hands. The walkthrough demonstrates this case.

This is also why a settlement suggestion is not a reservation on somebody's balance. Attach the snapshot revision so the UI can warn that a plan is old and request a fresh one before somebody pays. Once a payment has happened, however, its recorded fact must not be changed to match an old or new plan.

## 6. Correcting an expense does not undo a payment

Suppose Asha records a ₹90 dinner shared with Ben. Ben pays ₹45 and Asha confirms it; both balances become zero. Later they discover that the dinner expense should never have been in this group.

Deleting the expense and clearing its history would hide why Asha received ₹45. Instead, append the exact opposite of the dinner entry. The result is **Asha −₹45; Ben +₹45**: Asha now owes Ben the amount already received. No bank refund was performed. The application can suggest one and record its eventual confirmation through a new payment ID.

![Dinner creates a debt, confirmed payment brings both balances to zero, and reversing the mistaken dinner creates a new refund obligation without deleting the original payment.](learning-figures/lld-10-correction.svg)

The reversal references the original immutable expense. It does not rerun today's split rule or trust newly supplied shares. The same reversal ID is idempotent; a different second reversal for that original expense is rejected. An edited expense is a reversal followed by a new expense with a new identity. If a product promises that the replacement appears atomically, provide one command/transaction that stages both effects; two unrelated HTTP calls would expose the intermediate state. This example provides the explicit reversal and new-expense operations separately.

## 7. Simplify a route without claiming a minimum number of transfers

For a net-settled group, build separate collections of debtors and creditors. Take one of each, suggest the smaller of the outstanding debt and credit, reduce both and repeat. Our implementation picks member IDs in stable order so the same snapshot produces the same explanation.

```java
public synchronized Plan plan(String actor) {
    member(actor);
    var debtors = new TreeMap<String, Long>(); var creditors = new TreeMap<String, Long>();
    for (var e : balances.entrySet()) {
        if (e.getValue() < 0) debtors.put(e.getKey(), -e.getValue());
        if (e.getValue() > 0) creditors.put(e.getKey(), e.getValue());
    }
    var result = new ArrayList<Transfer>();
    while (!debtors.isEmpty() && !creditors.isEmpty()) {
        String from = debtors.firstKey(), to = creditors.firstKey();
        long amount = Math.min(debtors.get(from), creditors.get(to));
        result.add(new Transfer(from, to, amount));
        long debt = debtors.get(from) - amount, credit = creditors.get(to) - amount;
        if (debt == 0) debtors.remove(from); else debtors.put(from, debt);
        if (credit == 0) creditors.remove(to); else creditors.put(to, credit);
    }
    return new Plan(revision, result); // Advice only. No transfers or ledger writes.
}
```

Each step finishes at least one person's outstanding amount; the final step finishes two. With `k` nonzero members, there are at most `k − 1` suggestions. Building the sorted maps costs `O(n log n)` and the scan uses `O(n)` additional memory. The algorithm does **not** guarantee the fewest possible transfers.

For a concrete counterexample, A owes ₹4, B owes ₹6, C is owed ₹6 and D is owed ₹4. Member-ID matching suggests A→C ₹4, B→C ₹2, B→D ₹4: three transfers. A→D ₹4 and B→C ₹6 need only two. Both plans preserve everyone's net position. This is a defensible trade-off: simple deterministic suggestions versus a more expensive optimization or additional relationship constraints.

The original expenses remain unchanged regardless of the plan. A plan also cannot cross a currency or group boundary. If group policy requires direct original-creditor settlement, net balances alone have thrown away information required for that restriction; retain a pairwise obligation view instead of pretending the same solver satisfies it.

## 8. Interviewer follow-ups

**Why not store only “Ben owes Asha”?** A pairwise view is useful when original counterparties matter. This product explicitly chooses group net settlement, so one signed balance per member is enough for suggestions. Immutable expense/journal history still explains where every amount came from; we are not reducing the source of truth to an unexplained running total.

**Can two different payment IDs record the same transfer twice?** Yes, if both are proposed and confirmed as separate payments. Operation identity prevents transport retries, not false or duplicated real-world claims. Keep the same ID across retries/taps. A bank integration would additionally deduplicate its authenticated provider reference and retain reconciliation evidence. Manual two-party confirmation is not that integration.

**Does low lock contention make this safe across servers?** No. A Java monitor protects one instance. Multiple servers require an authoritative transactional group boundary or another proven concurrency protocol; putting the same maps behind two controllers does not create it.

**Can I remove a member with outstanding debt?** Not by deleting their identity or journal entries. This example fixes membership. A later lifecycle can disable new expenses while keeping the former member's debt, history, receipt permissions and settlement route under an explicit policy.

**Why not use floating point and round at the end?** It makes intermediate equality and independent share totals harder to defend. Integer paise plus an explicit remainder policy keeps the chosen currency exact. Percentage/weighted products use `BigInteger` before returning integer shares; no binary floating-point amount enters the journal.

**What would you add first in production?** Durable transactional storage, authenticated group authorization, bounded/idempotent command inputs, explicit dispute status and an audit/reconciliation workflow. Protect personal expense data in logs and restrict group reads. Only then add notifications or a payment provider through its own consent, identity and outcome protocol.

## 9. Closing answer

I separate split calculation from recorded group facts. Every expense freezes exact payer contributions and shares; the group atomically appends a balanced journal entry and updates an immutable-view projection. Payment reports wait for receiver confirmation, retries preserve operation identity, and corrections append reversals without rewriting money already received. Optional settlement suggestions preserve net balances and expose their snapshot revision, with no claim of a minimum-transfer solution or an actual bank transfer.

## Problem

Design a coffee vending machine that serves espresso, cappuccino, latte and so on. Each drink consumes ingredients (water, milk, beans, sugar). The machine takes payment, checks inventory, brews, returns change and reports low stock. It tests **State** (machine modes), **Factory** (beverages from recipes) and inventory consistency.

## Requirements

**Functional**
- Show the menu with prices. Only drinks with enough ingredients are selectable.
- Accept coins, notes or card. Return change or refund on cancel or failure.
- Deduct ingredients and dispense. A technician can refill and go into maintenance mode.
- Alert when an ingredient drops below a threshold.

**Non-functional**
- One transaction at a time; button presses while dispensing are rejected cleanly.
- Never keep money without dispensing: every failure path ends in a dispense or a refund.
- New drinks, recipes and payment methods plug in without touching the core flow.
- Recovers to a safe state after a power cut mid-transaction.

## 1. Scope and chosen solution

I implement one machine with a menu, ingredient stock, coins, cancellation and maintenance. `VendingMachine` serializes sessions; `Recipe` is immutable quantities; `Inventory` reserves resources; a `ChangePlanner` solves bounded coin selection. I keep device and payment results outside domain assumptions.

## 2. Vend algorithm and transitions

```text
select(productId) -> price and recipe
fund(sessionId,coin/payment) -> accepted credit
vend(sessionId) -> COMPLETE | FAILED | DELIVERY_UNCERTAIN
IDLE -> SELECTED -> FUNDED -> DISPENSING -> COMPLETE
```

Before DISPENSING I atomically reserve ingredients, a cup and change, then persist the vend identity. A ₹30 drink funded by ₹50 reserves ₹20 change before brewing. Known pre-brew failure refunds/releases untouched resources; after partial brewing I record consumed stock and waste, not a fictitious rollback. Device uncertainty blocks automatic repeat dispensing until reconciled. With 95 g beans, 1,000 ml water and 20 cups, a 10 g/150 ml recipe permits six servings. Maintenance rejects new sessions and records operator stock/cash adjustments without overwriting history.

The **recipe** describes ingredient quantities, **inventory** records available ingredients, and a **vend session** owns one customer's interaction. A state machine answers which actions are allowed at each stage: inserting money during maintenance must not accidentally start brewing.

## 5. Detailed decisions, algorithms and failure behaviour

### Key abstractions

| Type | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `VendingMachine` | Context and facade; holds the current `MachineState` |
| `MachineState` | `Idle`, `Selecting`, `PaymentPending`, `Dispensing`, `Maintenance` |
| `Beverage` / `Recipe` | name, price, `Map<Ingredient, Integer>` quantities |
| `BeverageFactory` | `create(type)` builds a beverage from the recipe registry |
| `Inventory` | `hasEnough(recipe)`, `deduct(recipe)` (atomic), `refill` |
| `PaymentHandler` | Strategy: `CoinPayment`, `CardPayment`; change calculation |
| `InventoryObserver` | Low-stock alert |

```java
class Inventory {
    private final Map<Ingredient, Integer> stock = new EnumMap<>(Ingredient.class);
    synchronized boolean tryDeduct(Recipe r) {
        if (!r.needs().entrySet().stream().allMatch(e -> stock.get(e.getKey()) >= e.getValue())) return false;
        r.needs().forEach((ing, q) -> stock.merge(ing, -q, Integer::sum));
        return true;
    }
}
```

### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Recipes as data.** Don't make `Cappuccino extends Coffee` with hard-coded quantities. A recipe registry (loaded from config) plus a Factory means a new drink is a config entry. Use a Decorator only for user add-ons (extra shot, extra sugar).

**2. Check and deduct atomically.** "Check, then deduct" as two calls races if two dispense paths exist. A single `tryDeduct` under a lock is all-or-nothing.

**3. When to deduct versus when to charge.** Order: confirm availability, collect payment, reserve and deduct ingredients, dispense. If dispensing fails, refund and restore ingredients. Never take money for a drink you can't make.

**4. State pattern.** In `Maintenance`, selections are rejected. In `Dispensing`, cancel is rejected. Each state handles only its legal events.

**5. Menu availability is derived.** Recompute which drinks are selectable from inventory after every deduct or refill, and push that to the display (Observer).

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** Inventory reservation, money acceptance and physical dispensing are separate observable steps.

Machine owns SlotInventory and a VendSession. Product describes a SKU; Recipe or Selection maps it to ingredients when appropriate. PaymentAcceptor tracks tender; ChangePolicy selects available denominations; DispenserPort reports success or jam. RefundJournal records incomplete recovery.

**Correctness rule:** Never decrement sellable stock twice for one vend request. Do not claim successful delivery before the hardware confirms it. Accepted money must end in purchase, change, refund or an explicit recoverable balance.

```text
slot(id,sku,available,reserved); vend(id,sku,price_minor,tender_minor,state); cash(denomination,count)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Select a product; validate stock and price; reserve stock; accept sufficient tender; verify change availability; journal the vend; dispense; on confirmation consume stock and return change; on jam reverse the reservation and initiate refund.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

Two sessions select the last unit while an operator restocks. One inventory owner serializes reserve/commit/release; restocking must not overwrite reserved counts. Network payment callbacks use the vend operation ID.

### Policy choices and extension seams

State is helpful for selection/payment/dispense transitions. Different products can use Strategy for preparation. Do not let product factories control cash or mutate global inventory.

### Edge cases that change the design

Enough total money does not imply the machine can make change. Power loss after dispensing needs journal reconciliation. A refund actuator can fail too, so expose an operator-review state instead of looping forever.

**Topic-specific cases:** Last-item race; exact change; impossible change; jam; duplicate confirmation; refund failure; restart during dispense.

### Work through the important decisions

Coin selection is a bounded change problem: available denominations and counts matter. A ₹20 difference does not prove a ₹20 coin is present. Reserve a feasible change plan before consuming money, or show an exact-change policy before purchase.

Once hot water has entered a cup, cancelling cannot restore the original ingredients. Record consumed quantities and waste; do not “roll back” physical brewing by incrementing every ingredient counter. Maintenance mode should block new sessions and reconcile cash/stock under an operator identity.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Does a new drink require another machine state?** Usually no. A new recipe changes data; new physical behaviour may need a new dispenser strategy.

**Can a failed sensor mean zero delivery?** Not necessarily. Distinguish known none from unknown.

## 7. Closing answer

I reserve a complete recipe and feasible change plan before vending. Session state distinguishes financial acceptance from physical delivery, and unknown dispense outcomes require evidence rather than an automatic second drink. Recipes are data; hardware and payment are explicit adapters.
