## Problem

Design the core of a cab-booking app like Uber or Ola: a rider requests a ride, the system matches a nearby driver, the trip moves through states, and both parties get live updates. This tests **Strategy** (matching and fares), **Observer** (notifications), a trip **state machine** and **concurrency** (a driver must not get two trips).

## Requirements

**Functional**
- Rider requests a ride with pickup, drop and vehicle class, and sees a fare estimate.
- Match the best available driver. The driver accepts or declines (with a timeout).
- Trip states: `REQUESTED → DRIVER_ASSIGNED → DRIVER_ARRIVED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, plus `CANCELLED`.
- Fare calculation, payment and ratings.

**Non-functional**
- A driver is offered one trip at a time, and two riders can never be assigned the same driver.
- Matching rules and pricing (surge) must be swappable.

## 1. Scope and chosen solution

I design request, dispatch, pickup, trip completion and cancellation. I choose one transactional service for trip ownership and driver availability; a location index is only a candidate source. Pricing and driver selection are policies, while Trip owns legal lifecycle changes.

## 2. Commands and authoritative transitions

```text
requestRide(requestId,rider,pickup,destination) -> Trip(REQUESTED)
acceptOffer(offerId,driverId,expectedTripVersion) -> Assigned | Conflict
arrive(tripId,driverId); startTrip(tripId,driverId); finishTrip(tripId,evidence)
cancel(tripId,actor,expectedVersion) -> Cancelled | Rejected
```

Acceptance locks trip and driver availability in a consistent order, verifies live offer ownership and expiry, verifies REQUESTED/AVAILABLE, then writes ASSIGNED and BUSY together. If either check fails neither state changes. A repeated accepted offer returns the original assignment. Cancellation uses the same trip lock/version, so it cannot commit concurrently with acceptance and leave an orphan busy driver.

Offers expiring later compare offer identities before releasing anything. ARRIVED precedes IN_PROGRESS, which precedes COMPLETED; cancellation fees depend on recorded milestones. The final fare preserves distance/time evidence and tariff version. A driver location from 15 minutes ago is excluded by freshness policy, regardless of its geometric closeness. If assignment is later split across services, I introduce a recoverable pending-assignment workflow rather than claiming two independent writes are atomic.

A *trip* is the lifecycle of one ride. An *offer* is an expiring invitation. A *driver lease* is temporary exclusive assignment while a ride starts. I begin with one dispatcher process and explicit timeouts before considering geographic partitioning.

### Build the transition model

Use trip states `REQUESTED`, `ASSIGNED`, `ARRIVED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`. Cancellation rules differ before and after pickup. Keep pricing estimates separate from the final fare, which may depend on observed duration, distance and policy.

## 5. Detailed decisions, algorithms and failure behaviour

### Key abstractions

| Type | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `Rider`, `Driver` (`DriverStatus`: OFFLINE, AVAILABLE, OFFERED, ON_TRIP) | Actors |
| `Location`, `LocationIndex` | Latest driver positions; `nearby(point, radiusKm)` (grid or geohash buckets) |
| `TripService` | Orchestrates request → match → offer → accept |

| `TripObserver` | `RiderNotifier`, `DriverNotifier`, `ReceiptMailer` |

### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Atomic driver reservation.** Matching reads candidates without locks, but *claiming* a driver must be atomic:
```java
boolean offer(Driver d) {
    return d.status().compareAndSet(DriverStatus.AVAILABLE, DriverStatus.OFFERED);
}
```
If the CAS fails, another trip took the driver, so try the next candidate. If the driver declines or the offer times out (a `ScheduledExecutorService` timer), CAS back to `AVAILABLE`.

**2. Location index.** A naive scan over all drivers is O(n). Bucket drivers by a grid cell or geohash prefix, then query the rider's cell and its neighbours. Updates every 3–5 seconds move a driver between buckets. A `ConcurrentHashMap<Cell, Set<DriverId>>` works in-process.

**3. Strategy everywhere it varies.** The matching policy and the fare formula change by city and product (Pool vs. Premier). Inject them per request type.

**4. Observer for side effects.** Trip state changes publish events. Notification, receipts and analytics subscribe. `Trip` stays free of push-notification code.

**5. Trip State pattern.** Cancellation fees depend on state: free before assignment, a fee after the driver arrives. That logic lives in each state's `cancel()`.

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** Driver assignment and trip progression need one authoritative owner even if location search is approximate.

Rider and Driver hold identities; Vehicle has capacity and category. RideRequest contains pickup and destination; Trip owns assignment and progression. MatchingPolicy ranks candidates; FarePolicy creates quotes; LocationIndex is a read model; DriverAvailability is authoritative for claiming a driver.

**Correctness rule:** A driver cannot be assigned to two active trips, and a trip has at most one accepted driver. A cached location result never establishes availability.

```text
trip(id,rider_id,driver_id,state,offer_token,version); driver_availability(driver_id,state,active_trip_id)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Create request; fetch nearby candidates; reserve one with an expiring offer; notify; atomically accept only if the offer is current and the driver is free; start after pickup confirmation; end with measured facts; calculate and settle fare using a durable operation ID.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

Two dispatches offer different trips to one driver. A conditional claim on driver availability plus trip ownership prevents both acceptances. Expiry and acceptance must compare the same offer token.

### Policy choices and extension seams

Sequential offers avoid many cancellations but increase wait time; a small parallel offer batch reduces latency while requiring atomic winner selection. Do not put map-provider calls into Trip state transitions.

### Edge cases that change the design

A rider cancels while a driver accepts. Serialize the transition and return the resulting state. Stale GPS does not prove pickup. Surge quote validity and cancellation fees require explicit policy versions.

**Topic-specific cases:** Only one trip claims a driver; expired offer rejection; duplicate completion; cancellation boundary; stale location exclusion; fare snapshot stability.

### Further reading

- [Java 21 concurrency contracts](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/util/concurrent/package-summary.html) — synchronization and executor semantics.
- [PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html) — database guarantees differ from process locks.

### Work through failure and extensions

A driver's phone may miss the assignment response. Repeating O9 should return the same assigned trip, not assign a second rider. Offer expiry must compare the offer identity so an old timer cannot release a later assignment. Location freshness also matters: a point from 15 minutes ago should not compete equally with a current location.

At larger scale, driver and trip records may live in separate services. A distributed workflow needs a recoverable pending assignment and compensation; claiming that two independent database writes are atomic would be misleading. I start with one transactional ownership boundary when possible.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Can two riders see the same nearby driver?** Yes. Search is a hint; only assignment establishes exclusivity.

**May the rider cancel an already completed trip?** No. A refund/dispute is a separate operation with its own rules.

## 7. Closing answer

I use approximate geographic search to propose drivers and one transactional assignment boundary to claim a driver and trip. Expiring offer identities, versioned trip transitions and immutable fare facts handle acceptance races without confusing a nearby point with an exclusive assignment.

## Problem

Design a food delivery system like Swiggy or Zomato: browse restaurants, place an order, the restaurant accepts and prepares it, an agent is assigned and picks it up, and the customer tracks it live. The heart of it is the **order state machine**, with **Strategy** for agent assignment and **Observer** for notifications.

## Requirements

**Functional**
- Browse restaurants and menus (with availability), build a cart from **one** restaurant, place an order, pay.
- Restaurant accepts or rejects. Agent assignment, pickup, delivery and live tracking.
- Cancellation rules that depend on the stage. Ratings for food and delivery.

**Non-functional**
- Only valid state transitions are allowed.
- An agent carries at most N orders. Assignment rules differ by city.

## 1. Scope and chosen solution

I support a single-restaurant basket, restaurant acceptance, payment, preparation and delivery. I choose an Order aggregate with immutable line/address/price snapshots; fulfilment, payment and delivery keep independent identities and state. Restaurant inventory and courier navigation are external services.

## 2. Submission and recovery contracts

```text
placeOrder(checkoutId,customer,acceptedQuote,address) -> Order
restaurantDecision(orderId,decisionId,ACCEPT|REJECT) -> CurrentState
recordPayment(orderId,providerRef,outcome) -> CurrentState
cancel(orderId,actor,expectedVersion) -> CancellationDisposition
```

Submission validates the quote and records order plus outbox event in one transaction. A relay requests payment authorization and restaurant acceptance using stable operation references. The workflow starts preparation only when its chosen acceptance/payment prerequisites hold. Duplicate callbacks are deduplicated and rejected if inconsistent with a terminal state. Kitchen acceptance after cancellation cannot restart preparation through this service.

```java
enum OrderStatus {
    PLACED, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY_FOR_PICKUP, PICKED_UP, DELIVERED, CANCELLED, REJECTED;
    private static final Map<OrderStatus, Set<OrderStatus>> NEXT = Map.of(
        PLACED, Set.of(ACCEPTED, REJECTED, CANCELLED),
        ACCEPTED, Set.of(PREPARING, CANCELLED), /* ... */);
```

### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Transition table vs. full State classes.** A transition map on an enum is compact and easy to audit. Full State classes pay off when each state has *behaviour* (cancellation refunds, allowed edits). A common answer is the enum for legality plus a `RefundPolicy` keyed by state.

**2. When to assign the agent.** Assigning at `PLACED` wastes agent time if the restaurant is slow. Assigning at `READY` makes the customer wait. Assign when `remainingPrepTime ≈ agentTravelTime`. That's a Strategy, and it's the trade-off interviewers want discussed.

**3. Price snapshot.** Copy price and name into `OrderItem`. Menu edits after ordering must not change the bill.

**4. Observer for fan-out.** Each transition publishes `OrderStatusChanged`. Notifiers, ETA recompute and analytics subscribe independently.

**5. Concurrency.** The restaurant's accept and the customer's cancel can race. Guard transitions with a conditional update (`compareAndSet` on status, or `UPDATE … WHERE status = :expected`), so only one wins.

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** An order coordinates restaurant, courier and payment states without pretending they are one atomic action.

Restaurant owns menu versions; Cart holds selections; Order snapshots item names, options, prices and delivery address. RestaurantOrder records acceptance and preparation; Delivery records assignment and proof of delivery. FeePolicy and PaymentGateway are separate ports.

**Correctness rule:** An accepted order retains the agreed price and items even when the live menu changes. A delivered order must not return to preparing because a late event arrives.

```text
orders(id,menu_version,total_minor,state,version); order_line(snapshot); delivery(order_id,courier_id,status); payment_attempt(id,status)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Validate menu and service area; create price snapshot; authorize payment; ask restaurant to accept; capture according to policy; assign a courier; record pickup and delivery; handle cancellation/refund through explicit compensating operations.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

A restaurant accepts while the customer cancels. Compare the order version and use cancellation rules for the resulting state. Deduplicate provider and courier events by event identity, not by matching message text.

### Policy choices and extension seams

Separate payment, preparation and delivery state axes avoid one enormous enum with impossible combinations. A coordinator keeps legal combinations explicit. Observer-style notifications may update views but must not commit payment state.

### Edge cases that change the design

One unavailable menu item may require partial acceptance with customer approval. An address change after courier assignment affects fees and serviceability. No courier available is a recoverable dispatch problem, not evidence that food was never prepared.

**Topic-specific cases:** Menu price changes do not alter an order; duplicate pickup ignored; cancel/accept race; partial item rejection; payment uncertainty; invalid address change.

### Further reading

- [Java 21 concurrency contracts](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/util/concurrent/package-summary.html) — synchronization and executor semantics.
- [PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html) — database guarantees differ from process locks.

### Work through retries and cancellation

The same restaurant acceptance event may arrive twice. Apply it once by decision identity and legal source state. An acceptance arriving after cancellation must not resurrect the order. Similarly, reassigning a courier should not create a second restaurant preparation request.

Cancellation before preparation may be free; cancellation after food is prepared may have a charge. Put this rule in a cancellation policy using recorded milestones. Do not inspect a stale UI label and guess whether the kitchen has started.

Keep network calls outside database locks. Record intent, call the provider, then record the result. If the process stops between those steps, resume using the original operation reference. A void and a refund are different actions depending on whether money was only authorized or already captured.

**Why snapshot the address?** Editing a profile after dispatch must not redirect an already accepted delivery without an explicit change operation.

**Does a payment success mean restaurant acceptance?** No. Financial and fulfilment outcomes are separate.

## 7. Closing answer

My order freezes accepted commercial terms and coordinates separate restaurant, financial and delivery states. Recorded milestones decide cancellation and compensation. Idempotent events advance only legal transitions, so duplicate acceptance or courier events cannot resurrect a cancelled order or repeat payment.

## Problem

Design a library system that can find books, lend physical copies, accept returns, renew loans and keep a fair waiting list.

Suppose A borrows the only copy of *Practical Java*. B and then C ask to borrow it next. When A returns it, the system must offer the copy to B. If B misses the pickup deadline, C gets a new assignment. A delayed timer for B must not take the book away from C.

That small story gives us the central distinction: the catalogue describes a book edition; circulation tracks who currently owns the right to a particular physical copy.

## Requirements

I will choose these policies for one branch:

- Search edition metadata by title, author or ISBN. A physical copy has its own barcode.
- A member has an immutable membership plan. Basic allows two active loans, a 14-day loan, one renewal and a 50-cent fine per overdue day. Plus allows four active loans, 21 days, one renewal and 25 cents per day. These are example policies, not universal library rules.
- Only active members may borrow, renew or place a hold. They must be below their loan limit to borrow or join the queue, and may borrow only one copy of an edition at a time.
- Holds are FIFO across copies of one edition at this branch. We do not substitute a different edition. Assign a healthy returned copy directly to the oldest eligible waiting reader.
- An assignment made on date D is valid through `D + 2 calendar days`, inclusive. After that date it may expire and the next reader gets a new pickup token.
- A loan is due through the end of its local due date. Renewal adds the original loan period to the existing due date, and is denied after it is overdue, after its renewal allowance is used or while another reader has an outstanding hold.
- Returning a book closes that loan and records any fine once. An inactive member may still return. Unpaid fines do not block circulation under this simplified policy, and the return never waits for a payment network.
- A damaged return goes to repair. It cannot be lent or offered for pickup until repair is completed.

The runnable example is an in-memory Java application with an injected branch clock. Authentication, payment collection, real messages, durable storage, lost-item billing and other branches are adapter or extension work; the precise transaction and recovery boundaries are explained below.

## 1. Begin with one title and one barcode

`BookEdition E1` describes *Practical Java*: its title, author and ISBN. `Copy C17` identifies the item on a shelf. If the library buys another identical copy, it creates another barcode, not another edition.

A loan needs both the copy and the borrower. **L1** means “A's borrowing visit using C17.” Later, C can borrow C17 under **L2**. The barcode stays the same; the loan identity changes.

| Object | Example | Why it is separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BookEdition` | E1, *Practical Java* | Catalogue metadata is shared by all copies of this edition. |
| `Copy` | Barcode C17 | Availability, damage and current ownership belong to the physical item. |
| `Member` with `Plan` | A has Basic | Policy values do not require a subclass per membership tier. |
| `Loan` | L1: A borrowed C17 | Due dates, renewals, return and fine belong to one borrowing visit. |
| `Hold` | H-B: B waits for E1 | B wants the next suitable copy, not a permanent claim to C17. |
| `Pickup` | P1: C17 is reserved for B | A specific assignment needs its own token and deadline. |

## 2. Keep one authority for circulation

`Service` owns the editions, copies, members, loans, hold queues and command receipts. The small implementation synchronizes circulation operations on this service. A desk or browser asks it to perform a command; it cannot edit a `Copy` array or keep its own authoritative free-copy list.

These are the public values used by the implementation:

```java
public enum CopyState { AVAILABLE, ON_LOAN, PICKUP, REPAIR }
public enum HoldState { WAITING, PICKUP, FULFILLED, EXPIRED, CANCELLED }
public enum Condition { GOOD, DAMAGED }
public record BookEdition(String id, String isbn, String title, String author) {
    public BookEdition { key(id); key(isbn); key(title); key(author); }
}

```

`Member` composes a `Plan`. The loan retains the plan values it received at checkout, so a future policy change need not recalculate yesterday's due date or fine rate. In this example plans and published views are immutable, and no plan-change operation is offered.

The system preserves these facts after every completed operation:

- An `ON_LOAN` copy refers to exactly one open loan; an open loan belongs to exactly one copy and member.
- Member limits are checked together with copy ownership, so two desks cannot each consume the same last borrowing slot.
- A `PICKUP` copy has one current member, token and deadline. It is unavailable to everyone else.
- The edition queue assigns readers in arrival order among those still eligible. At allocation, an inactive member, a member now at their loan limit or someone already borrowing that edition is cancelled with a recorded reason; the next eligible reader is considered. Rejoining later requires a new hold.
- A return receipt is identified by its loan. Replaying it cannot reassess the fine or modify a later loan of the same barcode.
- Fine assessment and ownership changes contain no payment or notification network calls.

A pickup reservation does not reserve a borrowing slot. Eligibility is checked again at checkout. A member who fills their limit with other books must return one before collecting the pickup, or allow its deadline to expire. This is a deliberately simple policy with an explicit consequence.

## 3. Checkout and renewal protect both member and copy

Checkout takes a stable request ID, a barcode, a member ID and, for an assigned copy, the current pickup token.

It first returns any matching saved checkout receipt. A reused request ID with a different payload is rejected. For a new request, it checks membership, current loan count and the one-copy-per-edition rule; then it requires an available copy or an unexpired pickup assignment belonging to this member and token. Finally it creates a loan and changes the copy to `ON_LOAN` together.

```java
public synchronized LoanView checkout(String requestId, String copyId, String memberId, String pickupToken) {
    key(requestId);
    CheckoutCommand previous = checkoutCommands.get(requestId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!Objects.equals(copyId,previous.copyId()) || !Objects.equals(memberId,previous.memberId())
            || !Objects.equals(pickupToken,previous.pickupToken())) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Checkout ID payload conflict");
        return previous.receipt();
    }
    Copy copy = item(copyId); Member member = member(memberId); LocalDate today = today();
    requireEligible(member,copy.editionId);
    if (copy.state == CopyState.PICKUP) {
        if (!copy.pickup.memberId().equals(memberId) || !copy.pickup.token().equals(pickupToken)
            || today.isAfter(copy.pickup.pickupBy())) throw new IllegalStateException("Pickup does not belong to this request or has expired");
    } else if (copy.state != CopyState.AVAILABLE || pickupToken != null) {
        throw new IllegalStateException("Copy unavailable or stale pickup token");
    }
    Loan loan = new Loan("L" + ++loanSequence,copy,member,today);
    if (copy.pickup != null) holdRecord(copy.pickup.holdId()).state = HoldState.FULFILLED;
    copy.pickup = null; copy.state = CopyState.ON_LOAN; copy.loanId = loan.id;
    loans.put(loan.id,loan);
    LoanView receipt = loan.view();
    checkoutCommands.put(requestId,new CheckoutCommand(copyId,memberId,pickupToken,receipt));
    return receipt;
}
```

Choosing C17 in search does not reserve it. Two desks may see it in a result, but only one authoritative checkout can change it to `ON_LOAN`. The other desk must read the updated state. The same lock also protects the member limit when the desks choose different copies.

The checkout receipt records the original checkout response. After renewal or return, use `loan(id)` to read the current loan, rather than treating a replayed old receipt as a new observation.

### A renewal is an extension of one loan

For a Basic loan issued on October 1, the due date is October 15. Renewing on October 12 extends the existing due date by 14 days to October 29. It does not create another loan or count as another borrowed item.

Renewal checks the actual loan, borrower, active membership, current date, saved renewal allowance and other readers' outstanding holds. A waiting hold and a ready pickup assignment both count as demand. The request ID prevents a lost response from extending the due date twice.

```java
public synchronized RenewalReceipt renew(String requestId, String loanId, String memberId) {
    key(requestId);
    RenewalCommand previous = renewalCommands.get(requestId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!Objects.equals(loanId,previous.loanId()) || !Objects.equals(memberId,previous.memberId()))
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Renewal ID payload conflict");
        return previous.receipt();
    }
    Loan loan = loanRecord(loanId); Member member = member(memberId);
    if (!loan.memberId.equals(memberId) || loan.receipt != null || !member.active
        || today().isAfter(loan.due) || loan.renewals >= loan.terms.maxRenewals())
        throw new IllegalStateException("Loan cannot be renewed");
    if (holds.values().stream().anyMatch(h -> h.editionId.equals(item(loan.copyId).editionId)
        && !h.memberId.equals(memberId) && (h.state == HoldState.WAITING || h.state == HoldState.PICKUP)))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Another reader has an outstanding hold");
    loan.due = loan.due.plusDays(loan.terms.loanDays()); loan.renewals++;
    RenewalReceipt receipt = new RenewalReceipt(loanId,loan.due,loan.renewals);
    renewalCommands.put(requestId,new RenewalCommand(loanId,memberId,receipt));
    return receipt;
}
```

We keep the policy conservative: an outstanding hold blocks renewal until that hold is fulfilled, expires or is cancelled during allocation. Renewal does not silently delete someone else's request to make itself succeed.

## 4. Return the item, assess the fine and serve the queue

A return targets **barcode plus loan ID**. Closing every loan for E1 would affect other copies; closing “whatever loan currently uses C17” would make a delayed event dangerous.

The return operation does this under the circulation lock:

1. Find that loan and verify its barcode.
2. If it already has a return receipt, return the saved receipt before touching current copy ownership. A changed condition under the same return token is rejected.
3. Require C17's active loan to still be that loan.
4. Compute overdue days and save the one return/fine assessment.
5. Close the loan and move a good copy to allocation, or a damaged copy to `REPAIR`.
6. Assign a healthy copy directly to the oldest eligible hold, or leave it available if nobody is waiting. Record a pickup-notice intent.

```java
public synchronized ReturnReceipt returnCopy(String copyId, String expectedLoanId, Condition condition) {
    Objects.requireNonNull(condition);
    Loan loan = loanRecord(expectedLoanId);
    if (!loan.copyId.equals(copyId)) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Loan belongs to another copy");
    if (loan.receipt != null) {
        if (loan.receipt.condition() != condition) throw new IllegalArgumentException("Return token payload conflict");
        return loan.receipt; // Old return replay cannot touch the copy's current owner.
    }
    Copy copy = item(copyId);
    if (copy.state != CopyState.ON_LOAN || !expectedLoanId.equals(copy.loanId))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Stale loan token");
    LocalDate today = today();
    if (today.isBefore(loan.checkedOut)) throw new IllegalStateException("Return date precedes checkout");
    long overdueDays = Math.max(0,ChronoUnit.DAYS.between(loan.due,today));
    long fine = Math.multiplyExact(overdueDays,loan.terms.fineCentsPerDay());
    loan.receipt = new ReturnReceipt(loan.id,today,condition,overdueDays,fine);
    copy.loanId = null; copy.state = condition == Condition.DAMAGED ? CopyState.REPAIR : CopyState.AVAILABLE;
    allocate(copy.editionId,today); // Record ownership and ready notices, never call a payment network.
    return loan.receipt;
}
```

![Returning C17 closes the specific loan, records its fine once and sends a healthy copy directly to the first eligible edition hold. A damaged copy goes to repair; payment happens separately.](learning-figures/lld-9-return.svg)

There is no call to `collectPayment` here. A payment service can fail or take seconds; the library should still accept its physical book and let the next reader use it. The fine is an amount owed, keyed by the loan ID, not proof of payment. A separate payment adapter can collect against that charge with its own stable payment ID and receipt.

### Calculate calendar days explicitly

Configure the branch clock with its time zone; the example uses `Asia/Kolkata`. [`LocalDate`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/LocalDate.html) is a calendar date without a time or zone, so the service obtains today's date from that configured clock. It does not divide elapsed milliseconds by 86,400,000.

For A's Basic loan:

```text
Checkout:        2026-10-01
Due date:        2026-10-01 + 14 calendar days = 2026-10-15
Returned:        2026-10-18
Overdue days:    max(0, DAYS.between(2026-10-15, 2026-10-18)) = 3
Fine:            3 × 50 cents = 150 cents = $1.50
```

The 15th is included as an on-time date. Return on the 15th costs zero, on the 16th costs 50 cents and on the 18th costs $1.50. Java's [`ChronoUnit.between`](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/time/temporal/ChronoUnit.html#between(java.time.temporal.Temporal,java.time.temporal.Temporal)) supplies the date difference. We count every calendar date, with no holiday adjustment, grace period or cap in this chosen policy.

Replaying L1's return on November 1 still returns the saved $1.50 receipt. It does not recalculate a larger fine using the retry date.

### FIFO holds need assignment tokens

The hold queue is keyed by edition E1. `allocate` removes the oldest eligible hold, then creates a fresh pickup assignment for a specific copy. A notice is only a stored intent here; actual messaging happens outside the circulation operation.

```java
private void allocate(String editionId, LocalDate today) {
    ArrayDeque<Hold> queue = waiting.get(editionId);
    if (queue == null) return;
    List<Copy> available = copies.values().stream().filter(c -> c.editionId.equals(editionId)
        && c.state == CopyState.AVAILABLE).sorted(Comparator.comparing(c -> c.id)).toList();
    for (Copy copy : available) {
        while (!queue.isEmpty()) {
            Hold hold = queue.removeFirst(); Member member = member(hold.memberId);
            String reason = ineligibility(member,editionId);
            if (reason != null) { hold.state = HoldState.CANCELLED; hold.reason = reason; continue; }
            Pickup pickup = new Pickup("P" + ++pickupSequence,hold.id,hold.memberId,today.plusDays(2));
            hold.state = HoldState.PICKUP; hold.pickup = pickup;
            copy.state = CopyState.PICKUP; copy.pickup = pickup;
            notices.add(new PickupNotice(pickup.token(),member.id,copy.id,pickup.pickupBy()));
            break;
        }
    }
}
```

Our example is now:

| --- | --- | --- |
| October 1: A checks out | Loan L1, member A | Due October 15. |
| October 2: B then C queue | Still L1 | Waiting does not end A's loan. |
| October 18: A returns | Pickup P1, member B | B is first; pickup through October 20. Fine $1.50 is recorded once. |

```java
public synchronized HoldView placeHold(String holdId, String editionId, String memberId) {
    key(holdId); edition(editionId); Member member = member(memberId);
    Hold previous = holds.get(holdId);
    if (previous != null) {
        if (!previous.editionId.equals(editionId) || !previous.memberId.equals(memberId))
            throw new IllegalArgumentException("Hold ID payload conflict");
        return previous.view();
    }
    requireEligible(member,editionId);
    if (holds.values().stream().anyMatch(h -> h.editionId.equals(editionId) && h.memberId.equals(memberId)
        && (h.state == HoldState.WAITING || h.state == HoldState.PICKUP)))
        throw new IllegalStateException("Member already has an outstanding hold for this edition");
    Hold hold = new Hold(holdId,editionId,memberId);
    holds.put(holdId,hold); waiting.computeIfAbsent(editionId,ignored -> new ArrayDeque<>()).addLast(hold);
    allocate(editionId,today());
    return hold.view();
}

public synchronized boolean expirePickup(String copyId, String expectedPickupToken) {
    Copy copy = item(copyId);
    if (copy.state != CopyState.PICKUP || !copy.pickup.token().equals(expectedPickupToken)
        || !today().isAfter(copy.pickup.pickupBy())) return false;
    holdRecord(copy.pickup.holdId()).state = HoldState.EXPIRED;
    copy.pickup = null; copy.state = CopyState.AVAILABLE;
    allocate(copy.editionId,today());
    return true;
}
```

Notice the order in `expirePickup`: check copy state and **current token** before looking at the deadline or changing anything. An old timer does not get to release whichever reservation happens to be there now. The same pattern protects a consumed pickup after checkout.

The scheduler must eventually call expiry for overdue assignments. Checkout itself rejects an expired pickup; it does not let another reader take it before the queue has been advanced. Delayed job execution can reduce availability temporarily, but cannot transfer ownership incorrectly.

### What changes when there is a database?

The in-memory lock protects callers sharing one service object, and the records disappear on restart. A durable adapter must commit the active loan, copy state, member capacity check, affected edition queue, return/fine receipt and notice intent as one circulation transition. Store command IDs and tokens with those records so a lost response or restarted worker can identify the original operation.

For this small single branch, serializing all circulation writes behind one database transaction boundary is a valid simple starting point. If later using finer locks, every writer must coordinate the member limit, copy and edition allocation consistently. Locking only the copy does not protect a member's last slot across two different copies. Locking only the loan does not protect renewal against a newly placed hold.

A database can also reject duplicate active loans or fine identities using its supported constraints/indexes, but the syntax and guarantees depend on the chosen database and transaction design. An ordinary `UNIQUE(copy_id)` over all loan history is wrong: it would prevent that copy ever being borrowed again. Do not infer complete circulation correctness from the phrase “use SQL transactions.”

Catalogue search can later become a separate indexed projection. Search may be stale; checkout must still consult the authoritative circulation state. This implementation uses a simple case-insensitive scan and makes no million-title latency claim.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Is the hold really title-level if its key is an edition ID?** It is above the copy level: any healthy copy of E1 can satisfy it. We deliberately treat one edition as the requested catalogue title and do not substitute a translation or later edition. If the product groups editions into a work, add a `Work` identity and define which editions satisfy that queue; do not silently use ISBN as both a work and a barcode.

**Why not subclass Member into Student and Faculty?** The behavior is the same; only values differ. A member has a Plan containing limits, duration, renewals and fine rate. Composition makes a new tier another value configuration. A separate policy interface becomes useful only when the rules themselves become different algorithms.

**What if the oldest waiting member becomes inactive or reaches their limit?** At allocation, cancel that hold with the reason and continue in FIFO order among eligible readers. This selected policy avoids leaving a healthy copy idle behind an ineligible request. A product could instead suspend the hold, but it would need a precise rule for when it re-enters the queue.

**May a ready notice free or transfer a copy?** No. The notice carries its pickup token and deadline. A sender can suppress an obsolete notice by checking the current assignment, but delivery success is not ownership. Only the circulation operation may expire or consume that matching token. A durable notice adapter should deduplicate by pickup token and retain an outbox record until handled.

**Can an old return clear the current borrower's loan?** Returning by barcode alone could do that. Here the loan ID is the return token. A completed old loan returns its saved receipt before any current copy mutation. A token for another barcode is rejected. Staff can display current ownership separately if an old receipt is replayed.

**Do overdue fines have to be paid before accepting a return?** No under our policy. Accepting the item, closing the loan and assessing the debt form one local circulation operation. Payment collection is separate and cannot prevent inventory from being returned. If borrowing eligibility later depends on unpaid fines, specify that policy and coordinate its authoritative debt state; do not add a slow payment call inside return.

**What about a book returned damaged or reported lost?** Damage is implemented: the loan closes, but the copy enters REPAIR and waiting readers remain queued until a healthy copy is available. Lost-item handling is outside scope; it needs its own inventory state and replacement-charge policy. Closing a loan alone must never imply that a missing copy is on the shelf.

## 7. Closing answer

“I would separate edition metadata, physical copies and borrowing visits. One circulation boundary checks member limits and changes copy ownership, while an edition FIFO queue assigns returned healthy copies with expiring pickup tokens. Returns target a loan ID, record the date-based fine once and move inventory without waiting on payment. Renewal respects other readers and reuses a command receipt on retry. The in-memory example demonstrates those rules; a durable adapter must preserve the same transition and token checks across commits and restarts.”

## Problem

Design Splitwise: users add shared expenses in groups, split them equally, by exact amounts or by percentage, see who owes whom, and settle up. The interesting parts are split types as a **Strategy** created by a **Factory**, a correct balance model, and **debt simplification** (a graph problem).

## Requirements

**Functional**
- Users, groups, and expenses with a payer (or multiple payers) and participants.
- Split types: `EQUAL`, `EXACT`, `PERCENT`, `SHARES`. Validate that they sum correctly.
- Show balances per user and per group, and "simplify debts". Record settlements.

**Non-functional**
- Money must be exact (no floating point) and balances must always sum to zero.

## 1. Scope and chosen solution

I record group expenses and manual settlements in INR. The product tracks obligations, not bank transfers. Expense snapshots contain payer, participants and exact shares; SplitPolicy handles equal, exact or percentage rules. Balances are projections that can be rebuilt from immutable records.

## 2. Amount allocation and settlement

```java
// Nonnegative minor units, validated distinct participants in stable order.
long equalShare(long total, int participants, int index) {
    return total / participants + (index < total % participants ? 1 : 0);
}
```

`addExpense(expenseId,payer,total,shares)` validates sum(shares)=total and currency before posting obligations atomically. For 100,000 paise split three ways, shares are 33,334/33,333/33,333. If the first participant paid, that person's net credit is 66,666; all group net balances sum to zero. Exact splits reject a nonconserving total, and percentage splits allocate rounding remainder deterministically.

`recordSettlement(settlementId,from,to,amount)` stores one immutable record and updates/rebuilds the balance projection. Reusing the ID with a different payload is a conflict. Corrections append reversal/replacement records. For simplification, I match the largest net debtor and creditor, transfer `min(debt,credit)`, reduce both and repeat until zero. This conserves net balances and uses at most debtors+creditors−1 transfers, but does not claim globally minimum transfers under every constraint.

## 3. Final design diagram

An **expense** is an immutable statement of who paid and who owes how much. A **settlement** reduces obligations after a separate payment or recorded agreement. A **balance** is derived from these events. I begin with one currency and integral minor units: ₹1,000 is 100,000 paise.

### Worked calculation and implementation

For an equal split, compute `base = total / count` and `remainder = total % count`. Give one extra paise to the first `remainder` participants in a stable order. For percentages, validate that percentages sum to the declared scale, then allocate rounding remainders explicitly.

```java
// Nonnegative paise; participants are already distinct and stably ordered.
long share(long total, int count, int index) {
    if (total < 0 || count <= 0 || index < 0 || index >= count)
        throw new IllegalArgumentException();
    return total / count + (index < total % count ? 1 : 0);
}
```

If Asha pays everything, her own share does not become a debt to herself. With shares 33,334, 33,333 and 33,333, she is owed 66,666 paise in total. Group balances sum to zero when every credit has its corresponding debit.

## 5. Detailed decisions, algorithms and failure behaviour

### Key abstractions

| Type | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `User`, `Group` | Membership |
| `Expense` | id, payer(s), total, `List<Split>`, `SplitType`, metadata |
| `Split` (abstract) → `EqualSplit`, `ExactSplit`, `PercentSplit` | user + computed amount |
| `SplitStrategy` / `SplitFactory` | Validates and computes amounts per type |
| `BalanceSheet` | `Map<UserId, Map<UserId, Money>>` or net balance per user |
| `DebtSimplifier` | Turns net balances into the minimum set of transfers |

```java
interface SplitStrategy {
    List<Split> split(Money total, List<User> users, List<BigDecimal> params);
}
class EqualSplitStrategy implements SplitStrategy {
    public List<Split> split(Money total, List<User> users, List<BigDecimal> p) {
        long share = total.paise() / users.size(), rem = total.paise() % users.size();
        // first `rem` users pay 1 paisa extra, so the sum is exact
    }
}
```

### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Money in minor units.** Use `long` paise or `BigDecimal` with fixed scale. ₹100 split 3 ways is 33.34 + 33.33 + 33.33, and someone has to absorb the remainder deterministically.

**2. Net balances, not a pairwise ledger.** Storing `net[user]` (positive = is owed) makes "how much do I owe overall" O(1), and simplification needs it anyway. Keep the expense list as the source of truth, so balances can be rebuilt (event-sourcing flavour).

**3. Debt simplification.** Split users into creditors and debtors by net balance. Repeatedly match the largest debtor with the largest creditor (two heaps) and settle `min(|d|, c)`. That gives at most n−1 transfers. A true minimum is NP-hard (subset-sum); the greedy approach is the standard answer, so say so.

**4. Factory plus validation.** `SplitFactory.create(type)` returns the strategy. Each validates its own inputs (percentages sum to 100, exact amounts sum to the total) before any balance changes.

**5. Edits and deletes.** Editing an expense means reversing its old effect and applying the new one. With balances derived from expenses, that's just a recompute.

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** Money allocation must conserve the expense exactly, including rounding remainders.

Group contains memberships; Expense records payer, currency, total and immutable shares. SplitPolicy computes equal, percentage or exact shares. LedgerEntry stores the resulting obligations. Settlement references the obligations it reduces, and BalanceView is rebuildable.

**Correctness rule:** The sum of allocated minor units equals the expense amount, and the signed group balance sums to zero. Currency is part of the value; amounts in different currencies must not be added silently.

```text
expense(id,group_id,payer,currency,amount_minor); share(expense_id,member_id,minor_units); ledger(operation_id,member_id,delta)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Validate members and currency; compute shares; allocate rounding remainder deterministically; persist expense plus ledger entries atomically; update the balance view; settle with an idempotent transfer reference; reverse corrections instead of rewriting history.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

Two settlements read the same outstanding debt. Lock or version the relevant obligation and apply only the remaining amount. Retry an operation by its identity to avoid double reduction.

### Policy choices and extension seams

Pairwise balances are easy to explain; minimizing transfers is a derived optimization and must preserve net balances. Greedy settlement is a useful heuristic but does not guarantee a minimum number of payments for all cases.

### Edge cases that change the design

Splitting 100 minor units among three people gives 34,33,33 under a stable remainder rule. Negative expenses need an explicit refund model. Membership removal does not erase debt.

**Topic-specific cases:** Rounding conservation; percentages total exactly 100; invalid members; mixed currency rejection; repeated settlement; reversal reproduces previous balances.

### Further reading

- [Java 21 concurrency contracts](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/util/concurrent/package-summary.html) — synchronization and executor semantics.
- [PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html) — database guarantees differ from process locks.

### Work through settlement and simplification

If Ben owes Asha ₹400 and Asha owes Cara ₹400, a suggestion may let Ben pay Cara directly. That reduces transfers while preserving each person's net balance. It changes the payment route, not the underlying expense history. Pairwise relationship constraints may forbid such simplification, so it is an optional policy.

Store settlement identities and amounts once. Two retries of the same recorded payment must not erase twice the obligation. Keep INR and USD in separate ledgers unless an explicit exchange-rate transaction defines the conversion; adding unlike currencies produces a meaningless balance.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Does rounding every share independently work?** No. Three rounded thirds can fail to add up to the expense. Allocate the remainder deterministically.

**Is a “mark paid” click a bank transfer?** No. It records a settlement claim unless a real payment integration supplies authoritative evidence.

## 7. Closing answer

I store immutable expenses and settlements in one currency, derive balances, and conserve minor units in every split. Debt simplification changes proposed settlement routes without rewriting the original expenses. Idempotent settlement identities prevent a repeated record from reducing debt twice.


### Design decisions & trade-offs

**1. Recipes as data.** Don't make `Cappuccino extends Coffee` with hard-coded quantities. A recipe registry (loaded from config) plus a Factory means a new drink is a config entry. Use a Decorator only for user add-ons (extra shot, extra sugar).

**2. Check and deduct atomically.** "Check, then deduct" as two calls races if two dispense paths exist. A single `tryDeduct` under a lock is all-or-nothing.

**3. When to deduct versus when to charge.** Order: confirm availability, collect payment, reserve and deduct ingredients, dispense. If dispensing fails, refund and restore ingredients. Never take money for a drink you can't make.

**4. State pattern.** In `Maintenance`, selections are rejected. In `Dispensing`, cancel is rejected. Each state handles only its legal events.

**5. Menu availability is derived.** Recompute which drinks are selectable from inventory after every deduct or refill, and push that to the display (Observer).

### Ownership, boundaries and invariants

**Central design question:** Inventory reservation, money acceptance and physical dispensing are separate observable steps.

Machine owns SlotInventory and a VendSession. Product describes a SKU; Recipe or Selection maps it to ingredients when appropriate. PaymentAcceptor tracks tender; ChangePolicy selects available denominations; DispenserPort reports success or jam. RefundJournal records incomplete recovery.

**Correctness rule:** Never decrement sellable stock twice for one vend request. Do not claim successful delivery before the hardware confirms it. Accepted money must end in purchase, change, refund or an explicit recoverable balance.

```text
slot(id,sku,available,reserved); vend(id,sku,price_minor,tender_minor,state); cash(denomination,count)
```

### End-to-end execution trace

Select a product; validate stock and price; reserve stock; accept sufficient tender; verify change availability; journal the vend; dispense; on confirmation consume stock and return change; on jam reverse the reservation and initiate refund.

### Concurrency and recovery walkthrough

Two sessions select the last unit while an operator restocks. One inventory owner serializes reserve/commit/release; restocking must not overwrite reserved counts. Network payment callbacks use the vend operation ID.

### Policy choices and extension seams

State is helpful for selection/payment/dispense transitions. Different products can use Strategy for preparation. Do not let product factories control cash or mutate global inventory.

### Edge cases that change the design

Enough total money does not imply the machine can make change. Power loss after dispensing needs journal reconciliation. A refund actuator can fail too, so expose an operator-review state instead of looping forever.

**Topic-specific cases:** Last-item race; exact change; impossible change; jam; duplicate confirmation; refund failure; restart during dispense.

### Work through the important decisions

Coin selection is a bounded change problem: available denominations and counts matter. A ₹20 difference does not prove a ₹20 coin is present. Reserve a feasible change plan before consuming money, or show an exact-change policy before purchase.

Once hot water has entered a cup, cancelling cannot restore the original ingredients. Record consumed quantities and waste; do not “roll back” physical brewing by incrementing every ingredient counter. Maintenance mode should block new sessions and reconcile cash/stock under an operator identity.

## 6. Interviewer follow-ups

**Does a new drink require another machine state?** Usually no. A new recipe changes data; new physical behaviour may need a new dispenser strategy.

**Can a failed sensor mean zero delivery?** Not necessarily. Distinguish known none from unknown.

## 7. Closing answer

I reserve a complete recipe and feasible change plan before vending. Session state distinguishes financial acceptance from physical delivery, and unknown dispense outcomes require evidence rather than an automatic second drink. Recipes are data; hardware and payment are explicit adapters.
